## BINDING PRINCIPLES (the 13b constitution, P-1…P-9 — copied verbatim per the architect-phase skill; the application notes in `.claude/skills/architect-phase/SKILL.md` bind with them)

- **P-1 — One capability registry.** Every user-facing act is declared
  exactly once: verb, scope (block/take/post/book), per-mode availability
  rule, its doors (menu / context / bar / shortcut / palette), and its
  refusal copy. Surfaces render their doors FROM the registry; gating is
  data, never per-view if-statements. A registry-walk test asserts every
  declared door exists and every rendered door is declared. This kills the
  D3/D4 class structurally — the capability matrix stays green by
  construction instead of by audit.
- **P-2 — Fire-and-DO (R115).** A rendered door either performs the act —
  including any mode-switch choreography, done visibly for the user — or
  delivers the operations layer's real refusal. A door that can never act in
  its context isn't rendered. Lecture-and-redirect is retired.
- **P-3 — One text surface, one liveness authority.** ONE TK2 surface
  component (live + read modes; one measurement brain — the width-gated
  remeasure; the shared canary) that every pane composes; the live editor
  core's identity machinery (`BlockRangeIndex`, crystallization) becomes its
  live mode, not a sibling implementation. "What text does this pane show,
  and is it live" is answered by ONE pure, unit-tested authority
  (`PaneTextAuthority`); views never re-derive it. Its invariants stated as
  code: exactly one live pane; the live pane is buffer-authoritative; read
  panes are projection-authoritative once caught up.
- **P-4 — Broadcasts carry signals, never decisions.** NotificationCenter
  may announce that something happened (mode changed, post opened); no
  listener derives business state or availability from a broadcast, and no
  flow's correctness depends on broadcast ordering. Cross-feature flows
  route through composition. (Stage 0's survey enumerates today's
  notification bus; Stage 1 disciplines it.)
- **P-5 — The friction doctrine (R116).** Repeated pushback against a rule
  marks an under-architected feature. The response is never another patch:
  write the feature's DESIGN MEMO — intended behavior whole, edge cases
  enumerated on paper, refusal wordings, undo story — get the author's
  rulings on the product-flavored edges, then build to the memo. Happy path
  ships first, but the edges are *decided at design time*, not discovered by
  the author. First two memos owed: **merge** and **the W ceremony**.
- **P-6 — The tutorial is the executable spec (R113).** The guided tutorial
  flow is the contract for every happy path. It stays green — automated
  where the harness allows, screenshot-walked otherwise — and any change
  that breaks a tutorial step is a regression by definition.
- **P-7 — Find bugs before she does.** Every layer keeps a proactive
  harness: the ops-layer soaks (exist), the board/view-model invariant soak
  (Stage 1), the registry walk (P-1), the per-batch scripted screenshot
  walkthrough. Red-on-old stays absolute: no bug-class fix without a pin
  that fails on the pre-fix code.
- **P-8 — Standing law, consolidated.** P1–P3 performance invariants bind
  the rebuilt layer exactly as they bind the substrate (invalidation ∝
  realized rows; one live editor; virtualized long views). Overlays, never
  rendering attributes. Chrome `fixedSize` texts always carry a `lineLimit`
  (the b6dae25 class). `Theme/VoiceControls.swift` is THE chrome vocabulary.
  Complexity lives in the store; restraint lives in the UI.
- **P-9 — No god objects in the frontend either.** A view model mixing
  board layout, text authority, and gesture routing splits by concern
  (`TakesViewModel` at 890 lines is the standing counterexample). Same
  hygiene rule as the substrate, applied where it was being waived.

## THE ARCHITECTURE ANSWER SHE ASKED FOR (her "whackamole" question, answered honestly at this kickoff)

Her words: *"index mode has presented more troubles than the other modes, even if I don't know the
underlying reason for that… just because it is a bit of a whackamole issue presenter… is NOT a
reason to just layer code on top and try to spaghetti our way out."* The honest assessment, from
the run records rather than from optimism:

1. **The underlying reason is real and known: Index mode is the app's only TWO-TIER EDITING
   surface.** Every block at rest is read-cheap SwiftUI `Text` (P3's own architecture — that is
   why a 214-block book stays snappy), and the clicked block upgrades to the one live TK2 pane.
   Every mode has ONE text engine; Index has TWO, and every defect it has presented lives at
   their SEAM: the click that carried no place (18l U0), the landing heights the lazy column
   didn't know (13u4 F3), the caret landing at the END (18l-b U4/#153), and now the metric shift
   (S3). The two-tier design is deliberate and stays — it IS P3 — so the seam is a permanent
   architectural feature that must be owned, not a smell to eliminate.
2. **The previous phases did NOT spaghetti it** — each fix landed with a traced mechanism, a
   red-on-old pin and a behavioural revert (U0's replica is pinned for fidelity against what
   SwiftUI actually drew; F3's heights come from the same `ProseHeightModel` brains other
   surfaces consume; the landing rides the ONE `LandingAuthority` all five surfaces now share).
   What the accumulated fixes DO reveal is the one remaining structural debt: **the two tiers
   never shared a single metrics source** (13u2 B aligned the leading x only; U0 named the
   read tier's constants but the live pane still keeps its own). Each tier answering "how tall
   is a line" from its own head is exactly why new whack-a-mole items kept being possible.
3. **So this round's U5 is the CONSOLIDATION, not another layer:** one named metrics vocabulary
   both tiers (and the click replica) draw from. After it, a metric drift between the tiers is
   a pin failure, not a discovery of hers. That is the "well built, smooth, and solid" answer —
   and it is the S3 row's own recorded remedy, waiting only on the taste call her report just
   made.
