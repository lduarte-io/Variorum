  **The answer she was given, and the shapes on the table (VX-1009 points here).** Search runs on a word index (FTS5, `unicode61 remove_diacritics 2`): a note's text is split into words of letters and numbers, and everything else — spaces, punctuation, symbols — is the gap between words and is not stored. So `+` has no entry to look up. Three shapes could make marks findable:
  1. **Exact quoted phrases.** `"1 + 1"` in quotes: the index finds the takes where those words sit next to each other, then the exact characters are checked in just those takes — cost stays proportional to matches (P1 holds). Needs one letter or number or more inside the quotes. The open edges are product ones: which marks make a phrase exact (math and currency signs only, or also `%`, `#`, `@`, `/`), apostrophes and hyphens (her notes hold curly quotes where she types straight ones, so a naive exact rule would stop `"don't"` finding itself), spacing (`1+1` against `1 + 1`), and which occurrence the passage line shows.
  2. **A lone symbol.** A field holding only `+` has no word to start from, so answering it means reading every take's text in the wing — a cost that grows with the library, which P1 forbids. Relaxing P1 is hers alone; a deliberate slower search (asked for, never automatic) is the shape it would take.
  3. **Changing what the index treats as a word.** Telling the tokenizer that chosen marks are word characters makes `+` findable but changes how ALL text splits (`a+b` becomes one word that `a` no longer finds) — every existing search changes, and the index is rebuilt.
  **Her first reaction to the three shapes (verbatim, also on VX-1009):** "seems complex - the fact that it would be a slower search doesn't seem appealing. and i don't like telling the tokenizer that chosen marks are word characters changes how ALL text splits provisionally - the fact that a+b wouldn't be found by a seems a bit unfortunate. so yeah, include those notes on the design ticket but maybe we don't end up changing anything at all. i guess everything is a tradeoff."
