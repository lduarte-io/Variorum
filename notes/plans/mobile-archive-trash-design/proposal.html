Archive & Trash on iPhone

Four ways the phone could separate Archive from the Trash, and finally give a note its Versions — mimicking what the Mac decided, in a shape that is actually a phone.

This is propose-only. Nothing here is built, and nothing here is decided. It exists because you reserved the decision:

“the phone will also have archive and delete separated and on mimicking the surfaces that the mac has decided on, in some way that works for mobile (make a note that it can have a design session and propose different designs to me with html when the time comes)”

Mobile batch 6 is split because of it: 6a and 6b build what was already ruled. 6c is this page, and it waits for you.

Ground rules — not mine to revisit

The five places

Everything anyone ever had is in exactly one of these. It is the tie-breaker for every call below.

  1. Where you left it — Shelf / Library
  2. Archive — notes, forever, no clock
  3. The Trash — what is leaving: notes · books · blocks, 30 days
  4. This note’s Versions — moments you saved
  5. This block’s History — its takes, savepoints, ancestry

And the standing rules: takes never row in a global Trash · purge doors (Delete Now / Empty Trash) live in trash surfaces only, never in History and never in the writing flow · confirm every time · the phone is a complete app, not a smaller one.

What the Mac actually did — because “mimicking” needs a target

Notice the asymmetry — it is deliberate, and it is the most useful thing on this page. Archive got a room. The Trash got an icon. That is the Mac saying archive is still your library, and the Trash is a utility. Every option below either honours that or knowingly breaks it.

How to read the four

They are ordered by how much of the phone’s shape moves — from the insides of one screen, out to the phone’s top level. Higher numbers are not better; they are bigger.

What it is worse at

One door still says these two are the same kind of thing — which is precisely the claim your ruling separated. A segment is a weaker statement than a room: you have to press it to learn the difference, where a room announces it. And the door needs a name that covers both, which is how “Archived & Deleted” got its awkward name in the first place. It is the cheapest option and the least committed one.

What it is worse at

It spends the phone’s two most crowded strips at once — the path line, which the code deliberately keeps two doors wide, gains a third. And the Archive door is still the last row of a scrolling list: to find your kept work you scroll past everything you didn’t archive. That is the phone’s version of the complaint that started all of this — “just going to the library you get a little lost.” It fixes the conflation and leaves the findability.

3c — the Trash. Unchanged from what is already built, minus the Archived section, plus a real title and promise line. The only screen in the app with a purge door on it.

What it is worse at

Three real costs. One: the filter row is at its width budget — four text doors plus a sort control at 390 pt is tight, and Dynamic Type, which this app has committed to, will break it before anything else does. Something has to give: the sort shrinks to a glyph (drawn above), the row scrolls, or the sort moves. Two: an archived note sits one tap from your live listing, so a mis-tap while browsing lands you in an unfamiliar list — a separate room cannot do that. Three: the word “trash” leaves the Library’s surface and becomes a glyph, and for a room visited rarely and urgently a picture is a worse door than a word.

All four can express all five places — with one exception worth naming

Option 4 can express them and still misreports them. Putting three places in the tab bar and leaving two in context is a legibility failure, not a capability one — which is exactly why its footer needs a sentence saying what is not in the room. The other three make no claim about the note-scale places at all, which is the honest shape: they live where their subject lives.

Independent of which option you pick

Two findings

One — a deleted book has no door from where books live

Books live on the Shelf. A deleted book rows in the Trash. In options 1, 2 and 3 the Trash’s only door is in the Library — so deleting a book from the Shelf means switching tabs to find it again. This is inherited, not introduced (the Mac’s trash icon is in the Library masthead too), and option 4 is the only one that fixes it by accident. The cheap fix works in every option: the Shelf’s path line renders the same 🗑 from the same door table — one declaration, two renderers, no new surface.

Two — the phone has two ⋯ menus, and the grain pairing falls out for free

The Mac teaches the two sizes of moment with two chords: ⌘S saves this take’s moment, ⌘⇧S saves this note’s. The phone has no chords, so that teaching needs another home — and it already has one, because the phone keeps block acts in the block ⋯ and note acts in the note ⋯:

block ⋯  →  Commit Take   +  Block History…
note  ⋯  →  Save a Version +  Versions…

Two menus, two grains, each holding its own write a moment / see the moments pair. Arguably a clearer teaching surface than two chords, and the pocket guide’s natural lesson. It costs the note ⋯ sheet two rows — question 5.

Honest limits

What does not translate from the Mac

  1. The side panel. Four of the Mac’s five recovery surfaces open beside the live editor. The phone covers the page with a sheet and stands the editor down. This is already recorded for Block History — the Mac’s “jump to the current block” door is meaningless on the phone and was correctly not ported. Same class for Versions: the Mac reads a moment beside the note; the phone must cover it, so Read is a push, not a peek.
  2. Hover. There is none, and every act must be a visible button. Not aesthetic: a menu row is a door no in-process test can drive — measured, its accessibility tree comes back empty — and this app has shipped dead purge doors under green tests before.
  3. Two search fields. The Mac’s Archive has its own search box beside the Library’s. Option 3 gives the phone one box, scoped by filter — arguably better, definitely different. Search scoping is 13o’s to inherit, so the answer should be recorded either way.
  4. The note ▾ menu. No menu bar on the phone. Its equivalent is a sheet of big tappable rows, which is why two extra rows cost more there than two menu items cost the Mac.
  5. ⌘⇧S. No chord culture on the phone. Save a Version has to be a visible row, and the grain pairing gets taught by the two menus instead of the two chords.

My call — yours to overrule

Option 3 — Archive becomes a Library filter; the Trash gets the glyph and its own screen

  1. It is the only option grounded in a rule the phone already applied twice rather than in taste. Pool became bookless; Blocks became scraps; Archived becomes archived. Nothing new has to be argued for.
  2. It says what you said archive IS. Your words: “archive is available forever and maybe still valuable even if you don’t want it in your current library.” A filter says still your library, differently scoped. A separate room says gone. The filter is the truer statement of your own sentence.
  3. It keeps the Mac’s deliberate asymmetry — Archive is a place, the Trash is a utility — without pretending two tabs are five sidebar areas.
  4. “Search it separately” comes free and correct, and hands 13o one scoping rule instead of a second search surface to reason about.

The honest caveat: its one real risk is the filter row’s width, which is a measurement question rather than a taste one — so it is question 2 below, and not something quietly decided.

If you dislike the filter idea, option 2 is the safe second: the most literal mimicry, no width risk, paying purely in discoverability. Option 1 is the cheapest and least committed. Option 4 is the one I would argue against — finding one is the only thing it has going for it, and finding one has a cheaper fix that works everywhere.

Over to you

Questions only you can answer

  1. Which option — or which parts of which. They mix: option 3’s Archive with option 2’s labelled Trash row is a coherent hybrid.
  2. Does the Library’s filter row take a fourth door? If yes, what gives — the sort shrinks to a glyph, the row scrolls, or the sort moves into the search row? Only your hands can judge it, and it gets tighter at larger text sizes.
  3. The Trash’s door: a glyph, a labelled row, or both? A glyph is tidier; a word is findable in a hurry.
  4. Does Archive get its own search field, or share the Library’s scoped by filter? 13o inherits the answer.
  5. Do Save a Version and Versions… go in the note ⋯ sheet? It grows a three-row sheet to five, and it buys the grain pairing.
  6. Should the Shelf get a Trash door too, so a deleted book is findable from where books live?
  7. The word. The Mac’s surface is Archived. Does the phone say archived or archive — and does the name “Archived & Deleted” retire entirely?
  8. Where does the per-note tray’s door sit? Candidates: a row in the note ⋯, or — the phone-native one — the existing blocks chip’s sheet gains segments in the note | archived | leaving: one subject in three states. flagged batch 6b may place this door before you read this page; if it does, that answer stands. The tray’s shape is not in question — it was already ruled.
  9. On a version row, is the tap “Read” with restore, promote and delete on the moment itself — or should the row carry its own ⋯? Four verbs will not fit a phone row inline.
