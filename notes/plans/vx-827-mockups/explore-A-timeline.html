A book read once is a rumor. only ever a rumor of itself — a report from someone you used to be, written in a hurry. Read twice, it starts to become a place: the corners you missed the first time hold the furniture of the argument, and you only notice them because you already know the room. That is why the books that matter wear out their spines unevenly.

Nobody teaches this. We praise the first pass — speed, retention, the clean sweep of a page — and say nothing of the second, where the actual reading happens.

Rereading is the quiet skill. It asks nothing of the book and everything of you: to come back, to be wrong about it twice, to find the room was bigger than you thought.
