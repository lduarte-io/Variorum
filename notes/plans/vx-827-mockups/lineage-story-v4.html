A book read once is a rumor. Read twice, it starts to become a place — the corners you missed hold the furniture of the argument, and you only notice them because you already know the room. That is why the books that matter wear out their spines unevenly: the same forty pages, returned to, until the glue gives. Nobody teaches this. We praise the first pass — speed, retention, the clean sweep of a page — and say nothing of the second, where the actual reading happens.
