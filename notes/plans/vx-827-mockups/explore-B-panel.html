On RereadingWriteTakesReadLensesCompareBlocks

Rereading is the quiet skill. Nobody grades it, and almost nobody admits how much of their reading is really rereading.

A book read once is a rumor; read twice, it starts to become a place. The corners you missed the first time hold the furniture of the argument, and you only see them because you already know the room.

That is why the books that matter wear out their spines unevenly: the same forty pages, returned to, until the glue gives. Nobody teaches this. We praise the first pass — speed, retention, the clean sweep of a page — and say nothing of the second, where the actual reading happens.

Which is also why a reread can feel like a small betrayal of the person who read it first.

Two directions on purpose: the family reads older above (your v1 words), a take's commits read newest on top (your reference's rail). If one direction everywhere matters more, the commit rail flips cheaply.

How this handles density

  • The face never grows. One pane, one rail, always — a block with one take and three commits and this one with five takes and 23 commits are the same size on screen.
  • A rail line is one line. Name or first words, and when. Quiet runs fold to a single line and open in place; the AI's pair stays two lines because it is the thing you came for.
  • Acts live once. Restore · Bring back as take · Compare · Name sit under the pane for the moment you picked — not on every row, which is most of what made v3 and v4 crowded.
  • Takes are rows, not cards. One row each in the switcher, archived in their own group, deleted as one line at the rail's foot. The text you read is only ever in the one pane.

Your calls

1 — one take's history at a time. The bet of this direction: the switcher swaps the rail, so "commits by take" means "pick the take, read its commits". Is that clear enough — or do you want to scan every take's commits at once? (The alternative keeps one rail but runs all takes down it, each take a tinted band — busier, nothing hidden.)
2 — landing after an AI edit. Drawn: when the newest thing on the take is an AI's pair, the panel opens on its "before", washed against now. Or it always opens on the take now, plain, with the pair just marked on the rail.
3 — the default seat. Drawn both. Recommended: beside the prose (Block History's seat), with Compare always widening to the full column. Or the full column every time.
