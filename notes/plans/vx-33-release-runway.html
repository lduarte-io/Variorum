Release runway

Six numbers to track, what waits for what, and the calls that are yours · 13 October 2026

Castle sets the date. Everything else can close in about two days if the five build runs start tonight. They're parallel lanes that share one stage, and the coordinator queues it.

Castle as designed is one sitting and then six phases in a row. The phases can't overlap, and only one custody effort runs at a time. At roughly a day each, that's close to a week after your decisions.

The go/no-go after Castle's first phase, which is headless and changes nothing in the app, is the designed moment to take the smaller "middle road" (about three phases) if the date matters more. That choice is yours. Shipping before Castle would go against your own reason for doing it first: "I don't want this mess to propogate once I have users in every conceivable configuration."

A sketch, not a schedule. It assumes everything starts tonight, about one implementer session per unit, the stage shared through the coordinator, and Castle's phases at roughly a day each.

What waits for what

  1. Castle: your Keychain check and D1–D10 → one merged design and plan → phase 1 → go/no-go → phases 2–6. While it runs, nothing else may touch Sync/, custody or the Keychain.
  2. VX-281 merges → you reword the MCP pane. Every other pane can be reworded any time, except Protection and Data Safety, which wait for Castle's words.
  3. VX-246's docs run last, after its own door work and after VX-281 merges, because they describe the final wire and the pane's tabs.
  4. The MCP guide page goes live before any build that links to it ships. VX-258 ships before any public build.
  5. Your tutorial session runs before VX-246 reaches its docs, or after it merges. Never at the same time: both edit the field guide.
  6. VX-216 comes after VX-284's other units, because they touch the same sheets.
  7. Merges go one at a time: first ready, first merged, and the next rebases.

  • VX-268's row calls: Pool and Book Panel rows (in), Blocks-scope rows (no), the open book (match the Bookcase).
  • AI opens Compare (VX-298): your own Compare already takes any note in the same wing, with no link needed, so the AI can open the same thing. One catch: two notes with no shared history open to empty columns, because “related blocks only” is on by default. I'd suggest: any two notes your picker would offer, with “related blocks only” off when they share no history, up to three notes, and link_versions' Open › opening Compare. Undecided: replace the open columns or fill the empty ones.
  • Claude Desktop (VX-299): never tested, and nothing blocks it. The “Any MCP client” setup is already the shape Desktop's config reads; it's installed here but has never been connected. (a) Out for launch; (b) one live round of about an hour, with your OK to edit your Claude config, plus a docs paragraph; or (c) that round plus a Claude Desktop setup tab, about half a day. I'd do (b) at least: writers who aren't developers are far more likely to use Claude Desktop than Claude Code.
  • Editing from the book view. VX-303 is your “it just doesn't work”: five book doors pass only the note, so they break the deep-link rule. It's small, and it's in VX-284. Your one call there: land in Write at the block (my pick), or in the note's last mode. VX-300 is the rest. I'd take its smallest useful version before launch (Index gets takes and a Takes sheet, plus Takes and “open in Write here” in its box, and Outline → Index keeps your block): one session, no editor risk. Keeping your place inside Outline and the full Index bar would come after launch.
