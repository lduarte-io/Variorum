Castle key cycle

What happens to every key when a synced library goes into Castle, and back out to Standard — step by step. The binding text is notes/plans/vx-266-castle-backups-prelaunch.md; the full map is castle-target-key-map.html.

A fresh key at every crossing. Into or out of Castle, out of an account, onto another Mac — plus Replace All Keys and the follow. A key is born Castle or Standard and keeps that kind for life: a Castle key only ever has a Touch ID item (or none, under Always ask); a Standard key only a silent item and an Apple lock. Into Castle, every older copy comes along. Out of an account, your copies come along too, and the account you left keeps only what came before. Out of Castle and onto another Mac, the new key starts clean: what came before keeps its own locks.

The cast

L — the library keyOpens your library file. A fresh one whenever the library crosses a line — into or out of Castle, out of an account, onto another Mac — or you replace your keys. Born Castle or Standard, it keeps that kind for life.
P — your passphraseIn your head. Opens a locked copy of the library key (and, synced, of the master), and the Castle anchor.
W — your twelve wordsOn paper. The same power as the passphrase — your way back if you forget it.
A — an Apple keyLives in iCloud Keychain, named by its own id; anyone in your Apple account holds it. One per library per Mac — this Mac's own, locking its Standard key file — and one per sync account, locking only the account's master in the vault.
M — the master keyOne per sync account. Opens the sync key and proves to the server that it's you.
C — the sync keyEncrypts everything sent to the server. The server never holds it unlocked.
— a Touch ID itemThis Mac's copy of a Castle key, released only to your finger — never to the login password. A Castle key only ever has this, or nothing under Always ask.
Silent itemThis Mac's copy of a Standard key, opened without asking. Keychain Access reveals it with the login password. A Standard key only ever has this, and an Apple lock.
Locked copy (a padlock)A key locked with another key. Whoever holds the second key gets the first. A key file is a set of these.
The historyIn the key file: older keys, each locked with the current key. Into Castle it takes everything; within a mode it grows; out of Castle, and for a library landing from another Mac, it starts empty.
The Castle anchorWritten first at every leave from Castle: the outgoing Castle key with its whole history, locked under one anchor key that only your passphrase and your words open. It rides in the key file and every later backup, and folds back in when you enter Castle again.
The protection recordA table inside the library file. The library's own facts, which every copy carries: its mode (the current key's kind), its account, and the family tree. And one section per Mac with that Mac's own facts.
The family treeIn the record, with no secrets: every key the library has had — its kind, which Mac made it, when it was made and retired. It decides whether a copy is this library's own.
This Mac's factsThe gate style, the idle lock, the scheduled export, the retired Apple key ids, the act in flight and the device identity. Two sealed homes — this Mac's section of the record and this Mac's memory — and the newer wins. Copies never carry them.
This Mac's memoryA Keychain item per library, no secret, sealed: which key is current, the mode and account last confirmed, and the second home of this Mac's facts. Before unlock it's only a hint and decides nothing.

The cycle, step by step

The whole cycle as a flow: what you have, what each step does, what’s new, what goes away, and who else can open your library after it. Both crossings make a fresh key, born Castle or Standard for life. Into Castle, everything older comes along; out of Castle, nothing does — what came before goes into the Castle anchor, which only your passphrase and your words open. Each pill wears the colour of its key; a struck pill is gone or opens nothing any more.
