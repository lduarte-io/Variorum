VX-266 — the first designer's review of the merged design, and its dispositions

What this is: the record of two review rounds of notes/plans/vx-266-castle-backups-prelaunch.md by firstcastleredesigner, the session that wrote the first design (design-proposal.md), at the author's ask on 2026-10-13. It changed no file. Round 1 reached the design's owner (variorum-08) through her; round 2 came back directly, answering the owner's proposed resolutions. Every finding is dispositioned below, and the design, the phase-1 plan and the target key map changed in the same commit as this file.

Her standing instruction for this work, verbatim: "DO NOT get into a game of whackamole - do not solve this issue in a way that brings back one of the other issues that your design resolved. … focus on a robust design that handles the whole situation thoroughly."

Round 1 (relayed by her, verbatim)

I read the new plan in full (notes/plans/vx-266-castle-backups-prelaunch.md), plus its phase-1 plan, the target key map and the second-opinion sections it relies on. I changed nothing and sent nothing.

Overall it's solid. The key map matches the plan, and the cold-read fixes are in, except the history-key exposure, which your rulings now accept with a stated warning. I found one glaring design error and one process error.

The glaring one: retiring the old Apple key reaches other libraries

Today's Apple key is shared by every Variorum library and account on one Apple account, on every Mac and phone (the plan says so in §3.5). Deleting it from iCloud Keychain deletes it on all of those devices, with no recovery. That's the second opinion's own fact.

The plan has Standard libraries keep using that shared key until they enter Castle. Its "one minter" rule only lets a new Apple key be made at sync setup, Use Standard, or a new library. So an existing Standard library never gets a key of its own.

So when one library enters Castle and answers "yes" to retiring the key, any other library on that Apple account that's still in Standard does this:

  • The current library loses its Apple-account recovery. It still opens on its own Mac through the Keychain item there, but nothing replaces its Apple key, so it sits in "waiting" forever. A new Mac or a wiped Keychain can no longer bring it back through the Apple account.
  • Its older backups lose their only road if it has no passphrase or twelve words. Standard allows having neither. If that device hasn't run the new version yet, its migration can't fold those backups' keys into its history either, because the key it would use is already gone.

The question's wording understates this. It says the other library "will keep opening it, but its own older copies will then need your passphrase or twelve words". For a library without those, its older copies simply become unopenable.

You're in Castle everywhere, so it costs you nothing. But the question goes to every user, and a Mac in Castle beside a phone or second Mac in Standard is a normal setup. Phase 1's scenario list doesn't include two libraries sharing the old key, so the test may not catch it.

The fix shape is your call:

  • give every Standard library and account its own key before the shared one can ever be retired;
  • make the question name the real cost, or ask the user to confirm they have no other Variorum library in Standard;
  • add that two-library setup as a fixed scenario in phase 1.

The process one: the D1–D10 numbers

The plan cites rulings as "D1"–"D10" throughout. That breaks your one-numbering rule from today: VX tickets only, and every ruling gets a ticket even with no work. D3, D6, D2 and D9 have tickets; D1, D5, D7, D8 and D10 don't.

Worth a look, not glaring

  • Rule 2 overclaims if the question is declined. It says "nothing reachable through your Apple account… opens your library or any copy Variorum ever made of it". If someone declines retiring the old key, pre-Castle copies still open that way; the plan's own prose admits it. Your new closeout rule about "every / all / ever" wording is aimed at exactly this.
  • The old per-library-ID Keychain items are kept "unread, for one release" (§3.13 step 4). A Standard library's old silent item has to be deleted when it enters Castle. Otherwise it keeps a pre-Castle key readable by anyone with the login password. The plan implies this but never says it outright.
  • VX-310: you said "there should always be a button". The plan drops the button when no finger is enrolled, and shows a reason instead when Touch ID is temporarily unusable. That's probably right, but it departs from "always", so it should go to you.
  • A narrow gap from retiring the account's Apple key. A synced device that joined silently has no passphrase copy in its key file. If it loses its Keychain before it follows the account into Castle (Migration Assistant, a reinstall), it can't open its local file. Its synced writing survives on the server; its unsynced edits don't.

Her direction on the numbering, verbatim: "i'm not particularly worried about the D- when it's just a decision made on one ticket (just add it to that ticket in the plan or in linear) but if the d ticket became a real work ticket, do add it to linear and make its actual # clear, the other session has a point there."

The owner's proposed resolutions (sent to the reviewer, verbatim)

  1. Retiring the old shared Apple key strands other libraries. a) The migration rehomes every live Standard library onto a key of its own: local-only gets a per-library key; a synced account gets the account key, and the vault's CAS picks the one device that mints it. Per-id names mean no same-item conflict. It only runs with a fresh vault read confirming Standard. It folds older keys into the history first, while the shared key is still readable. After that, an updated device depends on the shared key for nothing: its live library has its own key, and its older copies are covered by the history. A local-only Standard library never re-keys without sync, so even an un-migrated one opens its backups with the key in its silent item. The residue is old-build backups on a silently-joined device under a key retired by a Standard Replace All Keys, made before that device updates. b) Retiring the shared key stops being a one-time question in the Castle flow. It becomes an explicit act. Settings ▸ Protection always has it; Use Castle offers it when this library has copies locked with the shared key. Default: Not now. Its sheet lists the devices that have moved off the shared key. Each updated device writes a small, non-secret, synchronizable marker (device name and date) into iCloud Keychain; the list is information only, never a decision input. The sheet names the real cost: "Before you retire it, open the latest Variorum on every device signed into this Apple account. Any that hasn't keeps opening its library there, but its older backups without a passphrase or twelve-words copy could become impossible to open." c) Rule 2 carries an explicit exception until the shared key is retired, on every Castle surface: copies made before Castle still open through your Apple account, and on this Mac for anyone with its login password. Until then, the promise holds for every key the new build makes. d) Fixed scenarios: a Castle Mac plus a Standard local-only phone sharing the shared key, with the phone migrated and not; with and without a passphrase; retire, then check nothing is stranded beyond the act's warning.
  2. D-numbers. Every D maps to its ticket in the plan. D5 (seals) and D7 (rows owned by the library's account) are real work, so they get new tickets. D4 is withdrawn.
  3. Rule 2 overclaims if declined: covered by 1c.
  4. Old per-library-ID items left for one release: Use Castle deletes them for this library, along with the format-1 and format-2 key files beside it, after the fold. The custody law becomes literal: no silent item for any key the library has had, old names included.
  5. VX-310 "always": the button shows whenever a Touch ID item exists for the library's key, whatever the momentary availability. Pressing it with the lid closed or the keyboard away says why. With no working item (no finger enrolled, or invalidated), there's no button, just one quiet line ("Touch ID isn't set up on this Mac") and the passphrase road. I'll ask her to confirm.
  6. The silently-joined device whose Keychain dies before it follows: every key file that has a master wrap also carries that master wrapped under the twelve words, copied from the vault's recovery wrap. It's non-secret and 128-bit, so it adds no new brute-force target; I deliberately left out the passphrase-wrapped master for that reason. It refreshes while that master is current and is replaced only when the key file's master wrap is replaced at the follow. So the words always open the file, even after the master rotates and the account's Apple key is retired, and a backup's frozen key file gains the same road. No server change.

Round 2 (the reviewer's answer, verbatim)

Your six fixes mostly hold; below are where I could break them, by your numbers.

1a holds for live libraries, with three gaps:

  • Older libraries of OTHER libraries aren't live, so the migration never rehomes them. That covers Previous Libraries entries and adoption safety copies of a different library ID, plus hand-offs. A Standard-era one with no passphrase whose only road is the shared key gets stranded by retirement. Fix: at migration, while the shared key is still readable, give each such copy a per-key silent item on this Mac. Or have the retire act list every catalog copy whose only road is the shared key, and hold until each is opened or exported.
  • A window after rehoming. The newest backup pair still carries the shared-key wrap until the next scheduled backup. If the shared key is retired and that Mac is lost in that window, a never-re-keyed local library's backups can't be opened anywhere else. Fix: end the migration by writing a fresh pair.
  • Mixed builds. On an un-updated device, Re-link Apple account (AppleRelinkModel.swift:178 → healAppleHeldCopy → relandAppleHeldCopy) rewrites the vault's Apple copy under the one key it can read, which is the shared key. That undoes an account's rehoming, and a later retirement then strands the account's Apple road. The automatic reconcile arm won't fire after a rehome (key file id ≠ vault id); this manual door will. Fix: the retire act refuses while a fresh vault read, or any key file this device knows, still names the shared key's id. Add it as a scenario.

1b, two notes:

  • The sheet understates the cost. On an un-updated device the live library also loses its Apple-account recovery, not just its older backups. Suggested: "…keeps opening its library on that device. But if that device is lost or reset before it updates, that library and its backups open only with a passphrase or the twelve words — and without either, not at all."
  • The device list misses devices with iCloud Keychain off, because their markers never sync. Say the list can be incomplete. Also, her D3 words were to delete the key on entering Castle. Turning the shared key's retirement into a separate act that defaults to Not now departs from that, so confirm it with her. Per-library and per-account keys still retire automatically, so most of D3 stands.

1c holds, with one scope gap. "Any copy Variorum ever made of it" is false for a local-only library that also lives on another Mac (Send Library, no sync). That Mac mints its own per-library key for the same library ID, and this Mac's Castle entry doesn't retire it, so that Mac's Standard copies still open through Apple. Scope rule 2 to this Mac's copies. Make "delete on sight" match only key ids this device recorded as retired. Attributing by library ID would delete the other Mac's Standard key.

1d: add that Previous Libraries case (a different Standard library with no passphrase), the post-rehoming window, the mixed-build Re-link, and two Macs holding the same local-only library with one in Castle and one in Standard.

2 holds only if the docs then cite the tickets and drop the D labels; "D5" next to "VX-NNN" is still a second scheme. D4's withdrawal is a ruling too, so under her rule it gets a ticket or a line on VX-209/VX-266.

3 is covered.

4 holds, with one condition. Delete a format-1 or format-2 key file only after verifying its key is in the history. A key the fold couldn't reach keeps its file, because that file is the copy's only road. Example: a key wrapped only under a past passphrase she no longer remembers.

5 holds, but fix the wording. An invalidated item doesn't mean "Touch ID isn't set up on this Mac"; Touch ID is set up and it changed, which is the re-arm question's case. Use two lines: one for no finger enrolled, one for changed since you set it up.

6 holds; I can't break it. W already opens L directly, so M under W adds no new reach. One residue: if the words are regenerated while a silently-joined device is offline, its key file keeps M under the old words until it refreshes. If that device's Keychain dies first and the account key was retired, only the server copy recovers it, and its unsynced edits are lost. List it with your other residue.

Dispositions

The whole-situation answer to the glaring finding (design §3.5, "the old shared key"). The fix is not a better question; it is making the shared key unneeded before it can go:

  1. This build never writes under the old shared key — it reads it only to move things off it.
  2. Each device's migration moves everything it holds in Standard off it, while it's still readable: the fold first; then every other library this Mac holds copies of whose newest copy is in Standard gets a key file of its own (in Backups/<library8>/) under an Apple key of its own; then the live library moves to its own key (a synced account only with a fresh vault read, under the vault's CAS); then a fresh backup pair.
  3. An older build can put things back (its Re-link). The next device on this build moves the vault off again (a repair), and the retirement repeats every move before deleting anything.
  4. Retirement stays Use Castle's step — her VX-301 ruling's shape — after checks: the moves again; the route finder over every copy this Mac can see (other libraries' and other Macs' in a shared folder included) and every vault it can read; a vault still naming the old key stops the retirement; the sheet lists by name what would stop opening and says what the Mac can't see, in the reviewer's wording. Keep it for now is offered only when the list isn't empty; a store already in Castle gets one notice and a standing door. PROVISIONAL, hers to confirm — the reviewer is right that her ruling's question didn't show her the key's reach.
  5. Retiring records the key's id; delete on sight acts on recorded ids only.

Why this doesn't bring back what the design solved: no shared-name item is ever written (the F2/F3 races stay gone); every new key has a fresh id and an account keeps one through the vault's CAS (the second opinion's cold read, M1); no vault copy is orphaned, because a vault on the old key stops the retirement; the moves are executor acts and repairs, never a reader writing; nothing decides before unlock; and Castle's promise is kept by construction once the key is retired, with the one exception stated wherever it applies until then.
