One more, from the ripple survey. While checking the website privacy brief, the owner found that the design never said what the hardware marker is. A raw hardware UUID would have been a fair phase-1 reading, and the record — with every device section — travels inside every copy, so whoever opens a copy would read a hardware identifier for every Mac that opened the library. The reviewer agreed and strengthened the fix. Fixed: the marker is a hash of the Mac's hardware identity with a random salt the record carries, never the identifier itself (§3.2). It recomputes on the same hardware, after unlock where every decision runs, and a marker that leaks alone names no Mac. Phase 1's encoding scan asserts no raw hardware identifier is written. The random salt guards only a marker that leaks alone (a log, a Diagnostics dump, a support bundle), where the library ID would have been ambient; for anyone who can open a copy, the salt sits beside the markers, and the design claims no more. So, at the reviewer's addition, no log line, Diagnostics entry or support bundle ever carries a marker or the salt, and phase 1's executor tests scan the logs for it. Other Macs' sections stay in copies, since a rollback and a replacement Mac's adoption need them. The privacy brief's "a copy carries no device identity" stays true until the release switch; its at-the-switch delta is VX-329's to hand the site agent. It says that a copy carries, in its record, each device's random identity, a salted hardware hash and that device's protection settings, used only on that device.
