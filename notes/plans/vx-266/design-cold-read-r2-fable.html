Findings, most severe first

[MAJOR] 1 · Design §3.1 rule 2 (line 143–144), §3.4, §3.7 (the landing rule), §4.1 fixed scenario (768–771); plan §6 (Send Library scenario) · A Castle hand-off carries the sender's pre-Castle history out of the sender's control, and the receiving Mac's later leave re-opens that era to the Apple account. The design's own sentence — "A copy sent while in Castle exposes nothing" — is false, and the fixed scenario as written cannot pass.

  • The sequence. (1) Mac1 is Standard under L0, with pre-Castle backups (say in the shared iCloud Drive far folder — a §4.1 world state). (2) Mac1 enters Castle: L1 minted, L0 folded into L1's history, Mac1's Apple keys retired — its pre-Castle copies now open only through the history or their frozen P/W wraps. Promise kept. (3) Send Library: the pair carries the live key file byte-for-byte (LibraryHandOff.swift, confirmed), so L1's history — L0 included — rides to Mac2. (4) Mac2 lands it keeping the key it came with (her §1 landing ruling; landing is explicitly not a re-key, §3.4), in Castle. Still fine. (5) Mac2 later presses Use Standard (or joins a Standard account — §3.8 says that IS leaving Castle). The leave's new key L‴ is wrapped under a fresh Apple key, and its history "takes back the keys from before Castle" — which includes L0, the sender's pre-Castle key. (6) The Apple account (shared by both Macs in the ordinary one-person setup), and Mac2's login-password holder, now reach L0 through L‴'s history — and L0 opens Mac1's pre-Castle copies wherever they're reachable (the shared far folder).
  • Why it's outside every stated exception. Rule 2's third exception covers only a key shared "while it was in Standard"; the design asserts the Castle-send case clean ("exposes nothing… leaving Castle on either Mac starts a key that can't open Castle's" — true for Castle-era keys, false for the pre-Castle history the pair carried). Mac1's surfaces say its pre-Castle copies closed for good at retirement; Mac2's leave silently undoes that, and Mac1 is never told. §4.1's "the receiving Mac later leaving Castle exposes nothing of the sender's" would fail as specced — or the implementer must invent an unstated mechanism.
  • How her two rulings interact to reopen it. Round 1's M2(a) fix landed a different library "re-encrypted with an empty history" — which made the carried history a non-issue. Her landing ruling (keep the key it came with) correctly reversed the re-key, but the empty-history clause went with it and nothing replaced it. This is exactly the class the kickoff asks about: a ruling whose consequence the design doesn't honor.
  • Proposed fix (one of, her call): (a) a landed library keeps its key but its live history is NOT taken back by a later leave — leaving Castle on a landed library takes back only keys this Mac's own record saw enter Castle (needs finding 2's era/provenance marker); (b) the landing strips the carried history from the live key file (the frozen pair in the catalog still opens the sender's older copies on request, so her passphrase-OR-words guarantee stands); (c) keep the mechanism and state it — a fourth exception at Send Library (Castle form), the leave screen, and rule 2 — plus the fixed scenario re-worded. (a) or (b) preserve "in castle = safe" as said; (c) is the disclaimer road she has allowed elsewhere.

[MINOR] 2 · Design §3.2 key-file row (line 165), §3.4; plan §3 U1 (lines 139–142) · "History flows into Castle, never out" has no stated mechanism: history entries carry no era.

  • The format stores per history entry "fingerprint, epoch and retirement date" — nothing says whether a key was made in Castle. The record keeps no per-epoch mode log either. So the leave's "takes back only the keys from before Castle" and U1's "a key made while in Castle never enters the history of a key made outside it" cannot be computed from the stated data. The migration's fold (§3.13 step 6) compounds it: folded format-1/2 keys need an era attribution too (a workable heuristic exists — an old key file with an Apple wrap is Standard-era, without is Castle-era — but it's unstated). After enter→leave→enter, the second entry must also know it cannot reach the first Castle era's keys (they're not in the Standard key's history — correct, but only by accident of the data flow).
  • Fix: add a mode-at-birth (or era) field to each history entry and the current key's own record entry; state the migration's era attribution rule; this is also the substrate finding 1's fix (a) needs. Cheap now, PROVISIONAL-bait in U1 if left.

[MINOR] 3 · Design §3.4 (line 236), §3.1 rule 5 / rule 2 third exception (line 151), §8 residue, §4.1 promise 5 (724–726); the maps' who-table footnotes and CYCLE_OUT · Castle-era copies after a leave open only with that era's passphrase and words — a later change strands them, and no residue states it.

  • Castle's keys stay behind "openable only through their own copies' passphrase and words locks — for good" — those are the frozen wraps of their day. Sequence: enter Castle → back up → leave Castle → later change the passphrase and regenerate the words (Replace All Keys for a suspected leak — the words "the old die"). The Castle-era backups' only roads are now the old P and old W, which the user was told are dead. Nothing current opens them; the Castle-era master was replaced at the leave, so M is no road either. Promise 5's exception list (the floor; the sheet; older builds; §3.5a; rule 2's exceptions) doesn't contain this, so the generator will report it as a violation — or the model must count discarded old secrets as "held", which weakens the oracle. And every surface says Castle-era copies "keep opening only with your passphrase or your twelve words" in the present tense — false after the change (promise 9).
  • Fix: state the residue in §8 and promise 5 ("…with the passphrase or words you had then"), and let the route finder's words say which era's secret a Castle-era copy wants. Optionally, the regenerate/Replace doors could warn when Castle-era copies exist.

[MINOR] 4 · Design §3.8 (line 469–473), §3.1 rule 3; the map's ACCOUNTS paragraph · A Standard library leaving an account is never cut loose, and nothing says so.

  • Her ruling re-keys a CASTLE library at the leaving act "so its current key is never left under a master it no longer holds." A STANDARD library leaving doesn't re-key — so its current key stays wrapped under the departed account's master in every frozen pair from the synced era (decision 11: never rewritten). The departed account's master therefore opens not just "copies from while it synced" but the library's current key — the live library and every future copy — until the library enters Castle (there is no other re-key door for a local-only library: Replace All Keys needs an enrolled library, the key doors only re-wrap). The stated exception sentence is scoped under rule 2 (Castle) and is exactly right there; for the Standard case nothing is said at the act, and rule 3 names only the Apple account and the login password. A user who Moves a library off a compromised account X and stays Standard believes they've left X behind; X's master still opens everything they write after.
  • Fix: one sentence at the four leaving acts when the library is Standard ("that account can keep opening this library until you replace its keys or enter Castle"), in Data Safety, and a promise-9 word for the route finder. Or, if she prefers mechanism over disclaimer: re-key on leaving in both modes (the Castle re-key's machinery, silent item instead of Touch ID; the door's secrets requirement is the question — which is why this is a her-call, not a drafting fix).
