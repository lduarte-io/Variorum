The reviewers and the owner agreed one shape, and she ruled it:

  • her direction: "hmm I like the idea of landing the key instead of the history. this is what my opus session proposed to me - where do you agree and disagree? I want the system to feel very easy to understand to people - so a different library landing requiring a new key makes sense to me and is is similar to what I was thinking with rotate key at change into / out of castle, rotate key when you leave an account, etc. i don't really want disclaimers i want simple and clear system even if it is more onerous to switch between and switching makes your backups hard to open - that's fine. standard library leaving account - if they've forgotten their passphrase cant they just reset it if they're in standard anyway?"
  • her ruling, on the combined shape and on phase 2: "yes i agree and yes phase 2 will stay in debug builds until phase 3".

The shape (design §1, §3):

  • a fresh key whenever the library crosses a line — into or out of Castle, out of an account in either mode, onto another Mac;
  • leaving Castle takes nothing back: every copy from before the leave is sealed in the Castle anchor, under the passphrase and the words, which the anchor follows when they change;
  • keys born Castle or Standard and keeping that kind for life;
  • a family tree of every key the library has had, which decides "is this copy ours?";
  • one Apple key per library per Mac, and an account's only for its vault;
  • this Mac's facts in two sealed homes, the newer winning; copies carry only the library's own facts;
  • Use Castle checking everything at its door, and keeping today's export-off step;
  • a landing that takes one secret and has the other typed or made new;
  • every follow into or out of Castle typing one secret, once;
  • phase 2 compiled into Debug builds only until phase 3's switch.

The Fable reviewer's conditions for the agreed shape, all in the design: the zero-secret Standard landing; decision 33's stated asymmetry (a landing takes one secret; raising your own library keeps both); the copy-scoped leave sentence; the anchor's home and travel rule; the unopenable-anchor rule; the reset door's one ask for the words, with the double-forget corner as promise 5's one exception; and three lines for a follower's anchor — its words road is as fresh as that device's words copy, a words-typed follow never gains a master-under-passphrase copy, and the road through the Castle-era master is a birth road that any later door replaces.

The commits: the design 24a81aba; the phase-1 plan a4488456; the key maps and this record follow.

Opus's answer to her question

The six ideas, all adopted (with the Fable reviewer's agreement and her ruling): a key's kind for life; the family tree; the Castle anchor, in the version that takes nothing back at a leave; one Apple key per library per Mac; this Mac's facts kept at home; and Use Castle checking at its door, with the export-off step. Its process point — phase 2 off on main — is her ruling, and its readability suggestion is §3.11's one Opens with line per copy.

The ten hard-to-understand edges:

  1. Leaving Castle opens the live library. Said plainly in rule 5 and on the leave screen.
  2. Old backups needed the secrets of the day you left. Gone: the anchor follows the secrets. The one corner left is named by the route finder.
  3. The old shared key. Unchanged: asked once on each device; the sheet's hedges describe a past we can't see.
  4. A sent library wasn't independent. Gone: a fresh key at every landing.
  5. Which account opens which backup. The Opens with line on every copy.
  6. Touch ID after a macOS update. Fixed (m12).
  7. Standard on a device that can't see the Apple key. Now only silent setup waits. The device's own key file never waits, and the passphrase or the words open the vault.
  8. The login password in Standard. The field guide's try-it-yourself line (§3.11, phase 5).
  9. Markdown exports. Off on entering Castle (M6).
  10. Testers recorded as Castle by the migration's guess. The notice offers Use Standard as the key change it is.

Why none of this brings back what the design solved:

  • No key is ever written under a shared name, and after this change no key is held by two Macs — now hand-offs included.
  • No vault moves automatically, and the account's key dies only with the account's own rotation.
  • Plans stay beside the library and a missing plan never blocks; the act's identity lives in this Mac's device section, in two homes.
  • Prepare-then-commit keeps a road at every crash point, the anchor written first.
  • Every new mint is per id, at a deliberate act or the migration.
