Castle target key map

The system the VX-266 plan builds — and nothing else. This page is an acceptance artifact (her ruling, 2026-10-13): at the effort's close, every element below is checked against the code, and the checklist at the end records the proof for each. If a phase changes the design, this page changes in the same commit, at her word. The binding text is notes/plans/vx-266-castle-backups-prelaunch.md.

The two rules. In Castle, nothing reachable through your Apple account or this Mac's login password opens your library, or any copy Variorum made of it on this Mac or on a device of your account once it has followed. In Standard, your Apple account — and anyone who can sign in to this Mac — opens your library and every copy Variorum made of it. In Castle = safe. Out of Castle = not safe. The stated exceptions — a library already in Castle before this change, until it retires the old shared Apple key; copies from while a library synced with an account still in Standard; and a library sent to another Mac without sync before this change — are said where they apply, and below.

Castle

Castle — this Mac only

  • On this Mac: Touch ID — or your passphrase every time, with Always ask (then no Touch ID item exists).
  • Older copies: today's key opens the history, so every older backup opens with nothing more typed.
  • On a new Mac: your passphrase or words; the newest backup's history opens every older one.
  • Lose the passphrase, the words and every Touch ID item: the library and every copy are gone — said at the door, with Export a plain copy offered first.
  • A library already in Castle before this change: until it retires the old shared Apple key, its copies made under that key before Castle still open through the Apple account — one press in Settings ▸ Protection. Use Castle always retires it.

Castle — synced

  • On this Mac: Touch ID, or the passphrase.
  • A new device: sign in, then your passphrase or words once.
  • The server: locked keys and encrypted writing only; no Apple copy anywhere.
  • A device that joined without typing anything: its key file also holds the master locked with your twelve words, so the words open it even after the master changes.
  • A library already in Castle before this change: until it retires the old shared Apple key, its copies made under that key before Castle still open through the Apple account — one press in Settings ▸ Protection. Use Castle always retires it.

Standard

Standard — this Mac only

  • On this Mac: the silent item — nothing asked.
  • On a new Mac: this library's Apple key arrives through iCloud Keychain (when it's on — Variorum can't see it) and opens it — or your passphrase or words.
  • What it means: your Apple account, and anyone who can sign in to this Mac, can open the library and every copy Variorum keeps.
  • Other libraries' copies here: an older library, or backups, of a library whose newest copy is in Standard: this Mac's own key file for it, beside its copies, under an Apple key of its own — it opens through the Apple account as before. Deleting a library's last copy deletes its key.

Standard — synced

  • On this Mac: the silent item — nothing asked.
  • A new device: sign in to iCloud and Variorum: the account's Apple key opens the vault's master, nothing typed (when iCloud Keychain carried it).
  • What it means: the same as this-Mac-only, for every device of the account.
  • Accounts from before this change: stay on the old shared Apple key until they enter Castle or are re-linked.

The key history (both modes)

Every move

Entering Castle

  1. You type your passphrase and twelve words (set now if you have none). The screen says plainly: lose them and every Touch ID item, and this library and its older copies are gone — Export a plain copy first is offered. While the store still has the old shared Apple key, its checks run first and the sheet shows what they found (below).
  2. Synced only: a new master M and sync key C are made; your writing on the server is re-encrypted with the new C; the vault is rewritten with no Apple copy.
  3. A new library key L′′ is made and goes into its own item, made at this press (or nowhere, with Always ask).
  4. The new key file is written: L′′ · locked with P L′′ · locked with W (and L′′ · locked with M if synced), the old key added to the history — no Apple lock. Now any crash still leaves a road in.
  5. The record is written into the staged copy, saying Castle; then the library file is swapped to L′′ — the commit point. Copies pause from the new key's making to the swap, so every copy carries a matching key and record. This Mac's memory follows.
  6. The old key's item, every silent item of the library, the per-library-ID items, the escrow items and the old key files go — each only once the history holds its key.
  7. A fresh backup is made under L′′.
  8. The A Apple keys this library alone uses — its own and every device-own key — are deleted from iCloud Keychain, every device, no recovery, and their ids recorded as retired, so a copy that comes back is deleted on sight. The account's goes when the account itself enters Castle: its rotation removes the vault's Apple copy. Synced: each other device asks for your passphrase once, re-encrypts its own library, and records and deletes its own Apple keys too.
  9. Last, the old shared A Apple key is retired, so every older copy's Apple lock is dead, wherever the copy is. (There is no keep-for-now: to deal with something the sheet listed first, cancel and come back.)

Leaving Castle

  1. You confirm with Touch ID or the passphrase, after the plain warning: your Apple account — and anyone who can sign in to this Mac — will be able to open this library and every copy Variorum kept, including copies made while you were in Castle.
  2. A fresh A Apple key is made, with a new id. Only this device makes it; your other devices wait for it.
  3. The key file gains L · locked with A; synced, the vault gains M · locked with A.
  4. L moves from its item to a silent item; the record and this Mac's memory say Standard.
  5. Your other devices follow without asking anything; each adds its own Apple lock when the new key arrives.

Replace All Keys

  1. For a key someone may have used, or a device someone may have had: you type your passphrase and twelve words (or make new words).
  2. New M and C; the server's writing re-encrypted; the vault rewritten (in Standard the new master is locked with the account's Apple key — an account still on the old shared key is re-linked first, with a fresh key of its own — and Castle is the answer to an Apple-account intruder).
  3. A new library key, with the old one added to the history; every other device follows by typing a secret once.

Another device follows

  1. Another of your devices entered Castle or replaced the keys; this device sees the vault changed.
  2. It asks for your passphrase or words — once. Typing the old passphrase after a change elsewhere is refused with the change named: “Your passphrase was changed on [device] on [day] — enter the new one.”
  3. It gets the new M and C, re-encrypts its own library with a new key (the old key joins its history), and — into Castle — records every Apple key its key file names as retired (the account's, its own, the old shared one) and deletes its own.
  4. Until then it still opens and you can write; it just doesn't sync, and a banner says why. A follow into Standard asks nothing, and ends with a notice: your Apple account can now open this library and every copy Variorum kept of it, including copies made while you were in Castle.

Opening an older backup

  1. Inside Variorum, you pick an older backup of this library (or one is found at its place, say after a Time Machine rollback). Whatever unlocks your library comes first either way: Touch ID or the passphrase in Castle, the silent item in Standard.
  2. Its key is found through the history — today's key opens the older ones — so nothing more is typed.
  3. The copy is re-encrypted with today's key, straight into place; the library you had joins your older libraries.
  4. Your library is back as it was then, in the mode it's in now. A Castle library stays in Castle.

Other copies

  • A Castle copy of this library from another Mac, into a Standard library here: opening it runs Use Castle's own steps — your passphrase and words, a fresh key — or you cancel. Castle comes with a Castle library; a copy never lowers.
  • A copy whose key isn't in this library's history: the restore list and the confirm mark it “made on another Mac, or not by you” before anything happens.
  • A different library: it keeps its own mode and lands under a key of this Mac's own — re-encrypted, its history empty, so no key is ever held by two Macs; in Castle the landing asks for its passphrase and words. The confirm says so concretely; the library you had joins your older libraries. (PROVISIONAL, hers to confirm.)
  • A plaintext export: if it carries this library's ID it comes in under today's key and mode; otherwise it becomes a new library in Standard, and the confirm says so.

Existing stores, once (the migration)

  1. Anything in flight finishes first, with today's runner.
  2. The mode is worked out once with today's rules; a synced library takes the vault's word; anything ambiguous records Castle (with a one-time notice and Use Standard one press away); nothing Apple-reachable is written meanwhile.
  3. The record, this Mac's memory (sealed) and the device identity are written; per-key items added; old items left untouched for one release (entering Castle removes them once the history holds their key).
  4. Existing copies are attributed to their library and Mac; new backups go to per-library, per-Mac folders.
  5. The one key file is written and every reachable older key folded into the history — through the old shared Apple key while it's still readable, and the passphrase if needed (asked once). An old key file stays until the history holds its key; one the fold couldn't reach stays for good.
  6. Everything this Mac holds in Standard gains an Apple key of its own beside the old shared one — other libraries' copies first (this Mac's own key file for each), then a local-only live library — then a fresh backup, so the newest copy carries the history and every lock. Nothing is removed; a synced account stays on the old key until it enters Castle or is re-linked.
  7. A library already in Castle gets one calm ask: copies from before Castle still open through the Apple account until the old key is retired — with the checks, the sheet and a door in Settings ▸ Protection.

The old shared Apple key (stores from before this change)

  1. One key, every library. Today's builds lock every Standard library on an Apple account with one shared Apple key, on every device. Deleting it deletes it everywhere, with no recovery — so nothing loses its road through it before you choose to retire it.
  2. The move (each device's migration) only adds: everything this Mac holds in Standard gains an Apple key of its own beside the old one — a local-only live library, and this Mac's copies of other libraries (its own key file for each) — then a fresh backup. A synced account stays on the old key until it enters Castle or is re-linked: its vault has room for one Apple copy, and macOS can't say whether iCloud Keychain carried a new key. A device joining such an account wraps its own key file under a key of its own; nothing is ever written under the old key.
  3. The checks (Use Castle, or the door on a store already in Castle): the move runs again; then, with the old key taken away, every copy this Mac can see — other libraries' and other Macs' in a shared backup folder included — and every account vault it can read. They list what would stop opening, every Standard library or copy here with no passphrase or words, and any other Mac's key file holding this library's keys. A vault still on the old key — other than the account this act moves into Castle, and read fresh again at the last step — stops the retirement.
  4. The sheet lists those things by name, and says what the Mac can't see: give any library still in Standard on your other devices a passphrase or words first — one with neither, on a device that's lost or reset, opens elsewhere only if iCloud Keychain carried its key, or not at all if that device hadn't been updated.
  5. Retiring deletes the old key and records its id, so a copy that comes back is deleted on sight. Use Castle always retires it — there is no keep-for-now. A library that was already in Castle before this change gets one calm ask and a door in Settings ▸ Protection; until it's pressed, every Castle surface there says copies from before Castle still open through the Apple account.

New writing always belongs to the library's account, never to whoever happens to be signed in.

Leaving an account (This Mac Only, Stop syncing here, deleting it here, Move): a Castle library re-keys, both secrets typed, so its key is never left under a master it no longer holds. Its copies from while it synced open through that account while it stays in Standard — said at the act. The account's Apple key is left to the account. Joining or moving into an account in Castle is entering Castle; into one in Standard is leaving Castle, with the leave screen's sentence. (PROVISIONAL, hers to confirm.)
