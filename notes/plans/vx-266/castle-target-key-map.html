Castle target key map

The system the VX-266 plan builds — and nothing else. This page is an acceptance artifact (her ruling, 2026-10-13): at the effort's close, every element below is checked against the code, and the checklist at the end records the proof for each. If a phase changes the design, this page changes in the same commit, at her word. The binding text is notes/plans/vx-266-castle-backups-prelaunch.md.

The rules. In Castle, nothing reachable through your Apple account or this Mac's login password opens your library, or any copy Variorum made of it on this Mac — or, once it syncs, on any device of your account after that device follows. In Standard, your Apple account opens your library and every copy Variorum made of it since it came into Standard — through iCloud Keychain, when it's on — and so does anyone who can sign in to this Mac. Leaving Castle gives them the library itself, with everything you wrote in it, and every copy made after the leave; no copy made before the leave opens through them — those open with the passphrase or the twelve words you had on the day you left, and leaving never makes new words, so that day's paper keeps working. Your other devices each follow with one secret typed, so their own copies from before open with the words, and with the passphrase when it was the one typed there; each copy's Opens with line says which, and from which day. The library's key changes whenever it crosses a line — into or out of Castle, out of an account, onto another Mac: into Castle, every older copy comes along; out of an account, your copies come along too, and the account you left keeps only what came before; out of Castle and onto another Mac, the new key starts clean, and what came before keeps its own locks. In Castle = safe. Out of Castle = not safe. The stated exceptions are said where they apply, and below the who-can-open table.

The cast

L — the library keyOpens your library file. A fresh one whenever the library crosses a line — into or out of Castle, out of an account, onto another Mac — or you replace your keys. Born Castle or Standard, it keeps that kind for life.
P — your passphraseIn your head. Opens a locked copy of the library key (and, synced, of the master) — and, as it was on the day you left Castle, the Castle anchor (on a device that followed, when it was the one you typed there to follow).
W — your twelve wordsOn paper. The same power as the passphrase — your way back if you forget it. Leaving Castle never makes new words, so that day's paper keeps opening the anchor.
A — an Apple keyLives in iCloud Keychain, named by its own id; anyone in your Apple account holds it. One per library per Mac — this Mac's own, locking its Standard key file — and one per sync account, locking only the account's master in the vault. A Mac can hold more than one of its own for a library: Re-link's library arm mints one beside a key it couldn't read, and a replacement Mac adopts all of its predecessor's. Entering Castle retires every one; one left in an Apple account this Mac no longer uses can't be deleted from here, and its copies are a stated exception.
M — the master keyOne per sync account. Opens the sync key and proves to the server that it's you.
C — the sync keyEncrypts everything sent to the server. The server never holds it unlocked. On a device, the sync keys are held by what they can reach: the Castle way — a Touch ID item, or session memory under Always ask — while they reach a Castle-era key (in Castle, and through a leave's rotation until the previous corpus key leaves the vault), and in a silent item otherwise.

Silent itemThis Mac's copy of a Standard key, opened without asking. Keychain Access reveals it with the login password. A Standard key only ever has this, and an Apple lock.
Locked copy (a padlock)A key locked with another key. Whoever holds the second key gets the first. A key file is a set of these.
The historyIn the key file: older keys, each locked with the current key. Into Castle it takes everything; within a mode it grows, the anchor riding along unchanged; out of Castle, and for a library landing from another Mac, it starts empty.
The Castle anchorWritten first at every leave from Castle: the outgoing Castle key with its whole history (and any earlier anchor kept beside it), under one anchor key locked once with what opened that Mac's library the day it left — on a Mac that followed, with the secret typed at its follow as well — never anything Apple-reachable. It keeps the secrets of its day: never re-locked, carried unchanged by every later re-key and backup. It folds back in when you enter Castle again.
The protection recordA table inside the library file. The library's own facts, which every copy carries: its mode (the current key's kind), its account, and the family tree. And one section per Mac with that Mac's own facts.
The family treeWith no secrets: every key the library has had — its kind, which Mac made it, when it was made and retired. Two homes, unioned at open: the record, and a sealed mirror in this Mac's memory. Seeded at the migration with every fingerprint the catalog's key files name. It decides whether a copy is this library's own, so a rolled-back library file can't make its own copies look foreign.
This Mac's factsThe device identity and its hardware marker — a hash of this Mac's hardware identity with a random salt the record carries, never the identifier itself — the gate style (Touch ID or Always ask; a new device identity starts on Always ask), the idle lock, the scheduled export, the Apple key ids this Mac minted or followed and which are retired, and the act in flight. Two sealed homes — this Mac's section of the record and this Mac's memory: the newer wins, and the retired ids are the union. A Mac whose memory item is gone finds its own section by its hardware. Copies never carry them.
This Mac's memoryA Keychain item per library, no secret, sealed: which key is current, the mode and account last confirmed, the device identity with its hardware marker, a salted hash (on other hardware a new identity is minted), a mirror of the key file and of the family tree, and the second home of this Mac's facts. Deleting it changes nothing on its own: the Mac finds its own section by its hardware. Before unlock it's only a hint and decides nothing.

Castle

Castle — this Mac only

  • On this Mac: Touch ID — or your passphrase every time, with Always ask (then no Touch ID item exists).
  • Older copies: today's key opens the history, which took everything when the library entered Castle — a Castle anchor from an earlier leave folded back in — so every older backup opens with nothing more typed. An anchor the typed secrets couldn't open (they had changed since that leave) was kept beside the history, unchanged: its copies open with the secrets of its day, and Use Castle's door said which secrets, of which day.
  • On a new Mac: your passphrase or words; the newest backup's history opens every older one.
  • Sent to another Mac: it lands there under a fresh key with an empty history — nothing is shared. Your passphrase alone, or your words alone, unlocks the pair, and the landing asks for the other one, typed or made new. Either Mac entering or leaving Castle later changes nothing on the other.
  • Lose the passphrase, the words and every Touch ID item: the library and every copy are gone — said at the door, with Export a plain copy offered first.
  • A library already in Castle before this change: until it retires the old shared Apple key, its copies made under that key before Castle still open through the Apple account — asked once on each device, with a door in Settings ▸ Protection that goes once pressed. A library entering Castle after this change retires it as Use Castle's last step.

Standard

Standard — this Mac only

  • On this Mac: the silent item — nothing asked.
  • On a new Mac: this Mac's own Apple key for the library arrives through iCloud Keychain (when it's on — Variorum can't see it) and opens the newest backup's key file — or your passphrase or words.
  • What it means: your Apple account, and anyone who can sign in to this Mac, open the library and every copy Variorum made of it since it came into Standard.
  • After leaving Castle: that means the library itself — with everything written in it, in Castle too — and every copy made after the leave. No copy made before the leave opens through them: those open with the passphrase or the twelve words you had on the day you left, through the Castle anchor. Leaving never makes new words, so that day's paper keeps working. Your other devices each follow with one secret typed, so their own copies from before open with the words, and with the passphrase when it was the one you typed there to follow; each copy's Opens with line says which, and from which day. A Castle-era copy you open into the library is known by its family tree, takes Standard, and the confirm says its writing will then open through the Apple account.
  • Your passphrase and words: changing the passphrase, or making new words, re-locks the current key and nothing else. The Castle anchor keeps the secrets of the day you left: it was locked once, when it was written, is never re-locked, and every later re-key carries it unchanged. While there's an anchor, every door that replaces a secret says what still opens the copies from before the leave: new words, Replace All Keys, a leave from an account and sync setup's new words say “Copies from before 12 October still open with the words you're replacing — keep that paper.”; change passphrase says “Copies from before 12 October still open with the passphrase you're replacing, or that day's words.”; and the reset road for a forgotten passphrase says “Copies from before 12 October now open with that day's twelve words — keep that paper.”
  • Other libraries' copies here: an older library, or backups, of a library whose newest copy is in Standard: this Mac's own key file for it, beside its copies, under an Apple key of its own — it opens through the Apple account as before. Deleting a library's last copy deletes its key file and its Apple key.
  • Sent to another Mac: it lands there under a fresh key with an empty history — nothing is shared. If that Mac can read the Apple key that opens the pair, it lands with nothing typed, under an Apple key of that Mac's own, and the passphrase and words are offered as Standard offers them — declined, the confirm says the landed library opens through that Mac and its Apple account only. Otherwise one secret unlocks it and the other is typed or made new.
  • If this Mac's own Apple key is lost: an iCloud Keychain reset, or a change of Apple ID: no repair mints a new one. The assessment says the Apple road is lost and offers Re-link's library arm, which mints a fresh own key for this Mac and re-locks the key file under it — and every other Standard key file here that needs one, other libraries' included — re-keying nothing. Local-only libraries get it too. After a change of Apple ID, copies under the old key keep opening through the former account, for as long as its iCloud Keychain keeps the key — this Mac can't delete it there; a stated exception, said on their Opens with line.

Standard — synced

  • On this Mac: the silent item — nothing asked.
  • A new device: sign in to iCloud and Variorum: the account's Apple key opens the vault's master, nothing typed (when iCloud Keychain carried it). The device then locks its own key file under an Apple key of its own.
  • What it means: the same as this-Mac-only, for every device of the account. The account's Apple key locks only the master in the vault; each device's key file is under that device's own.
  • Leaving the account: the library re-keys: a new key with its silent item, locked under this Mac's existing own Apple key — nothing is minted, as for Replace All Keys, since the account's key never locks the library; the passphrase typed or set new, the words typed or made new. The new key's history takes the old one, and any anchor rides along unchanged. The account you left opens only the copies from while it synced in Standard — until it replaces its keys or enters Castle — never what you write after. The account's Apple key is left to its other devices.
  • Sent to another Mac: as this-Mac-only — and a synced pair lands held, as today: a secret made new at its landing becomes the account's at Resume Sync, through the account's own forgot road with the secret just typed, and the landing says so.
  • If this Mac's own Apple key is lost: an iCloud Keychain reset, or a change of Apple ID: no repair mints a new one. The assessment says the Apple road is lost and offers Re-link's library arm, which mints a fresh own key for this Mac and re-locks the key file under it — and every other Standard key file here that needs one, other libraries' included — re-keying nothing. Local-only libraries get it too. After a change of Apple ID, copies under the old key keep opening through the former account, for as long as its iCloud Keychain keeps the key — this Mac can't delete it there; a stated exception, said on their Opens with line. The account's own Apple key, when no device can read it, is Re-link's: a fresh account key, landed under the vault's compare-and-set.
  • Accounts from before this change: their vault stays on the old shared Apple key until they enter Castle or are re-linked; nothing is written under it, and each device locks its own key file under a key of its own.
