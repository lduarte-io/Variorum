Castle key map

What each key is, where it lives, what it's locked with, and what the server holds — today, and in the new design (your rulings of 2026-10-13, and round 3's of 2026-10-14). A companion to second-opinion.html; the build target alone is castle-target-key-map.html.

The whole idea. In Castle, nothing reachable through your Apple account or this Mac's login password opens your library, or any copy Variorum made of it on this Mac — or, once it syncs, on any device of your account after that device follows. In Standard, your Apple account opens your library and every copy Variorum made of it since it came into Standard — through iCloud Keychain, when it's on — and so does anyone who can sign in to this Mac. Leaving Castle gives them the library itself, with everything you wrote in it, and every copy made after the leave; no copy made before the leave opens through them — those open with the passphrase or the twelve words you had on the day you left, and leaving never makes new words, so that day's paper keeps working. Your other devices each follow with one secret typed, so their own copies from before open with the words, and with the passphrase when it was the one typed there; each copy's Opens with line says which, and from which day. The library's key changes whenever it crosses a line — into or out of Castle, out of an account, onto another Mac: into Castle, every older copy comes along; out of an account, your copies come along too, and the account you left keeps only what came before; out of Castle and onto another Mac, the new key starts clean, and what came before keeps its own locks. In Castle = safe. Out of Castle = not safe. The stated exceptions are listed at the end.

The cast

Every key is a random 256-bit number except your passphrase and words, which you hold. A locked copy is one key locked with another; the padlock's colour says which key opens it. Nothing on the server — and nothing in a key file — opens anything by itself.

L — the library keyOpens your library file. A fresh one whenever the library crosses a line — into or out of Castle, out of an account, onto another Mac — or you replace your keys. Born Castle or Standard, it keeps that kind for life.
P — your passphraseIn your head. Opens a locked copy of the library key (and, synced, of the master) — and, as it was on the day you left Castle, the Castle anchor (on a device that followed, when it was the one you typed there to follow).
W — your twelve wordsOn paper. The same power as the passphrase — your way back if you forget it. Leaving Castle never makes new words, so that day's paper keeps opening the anchor.
A — an Apple keyLives in iCloud Keychain, named by its own id; anyone in your Apple account holds it. One per library per Mac — this Mac's own, locking its Standard key file — and one per sync account, locking only the account's master in the vault. A Mac can hold more than one of its own for a library: Re-link's library arm mints one beside a key it couldn't read, and a replacement Mac adopts all of its predecessor's. Entering Castle retires every one; one left in an Apple account this Mac no longer uses can't be deleted from here, and its copies are a stated exception.
M — the master keyOne per sync account. Opens the sync key and proves to the server that it's you.
C — the sync keyEncrypts everything sent to the server. The server never holds it unlocked. On a device, the sync keys are held by what they can reach: the Castle way — a Touch ID item, or session memory under Always ask — while they reach a Castle-era key (in Castle, and through a leave's rotation until the previous corpus key leaves the vault), and in a silent item otherwise.
— a Touch ID itemThis Mac's copy of a Castle key, released only to your finger — never to the login password. A Castle key only ever has this, or nothing under Always ask.
Silent itemThis Mac's copy of a Standard key, opened without asking. Keychain Access reveals it with the login password. A Standard key only ever has this, and an Apple lock.
Locked copy (a padlock)A key locked with another key. Whoever holds the second key gets the first. A key file is a set of these.
The historyIn the key file: older keys, each locked with the current key. Into Castle it takes everything; within a mode it grows, the anchor riding along unchanged; out of Castle, and for a library landing from another Mac, it starts empty.
The Castle anchorWritten first at every leave from Castle: the outgoing Castle key with its whole history (and any earlier anchor kept beside it), under one anchor key locked once with what opened that Mac's library the day it left — on a Mac that followed, with the secret typed at its follow as well — never anything Apple-reachable. It keeps the secrets of its day: never re-locked, carried unchanged by every later re-key and backup. It folds back in when you enter Castle again.
The protection recordA table inside the library file. The library's own facts, which every copy carries: its mode (the current key's kind), its account, and the family tree. And one section per Mac with that Mac's own facts.
The family treeWith no secrets: every key the library has had — its kind, which Mac made it, when it was made and retired. Two homes, unioned at open: the record, and a sealed mirror in this Mac's memory. Seeded at the migration with every fingerprint the catalog's key files name. It decides whether a copy is this library's own, so a rolled-back library file can't make its own copies look foreign.
This Mac's factsThe device identity and its hardware marker — a hash of this Mac's hardware identity with a random salt the record carries, never the identifier itself — the gate style (Touch ID or Always ask; a new device identity starts on Always ask), the idle lock, the scheduled export, the Apple key ids this Mac minted or followed and which are retired, and the act in flight. Two sealed homes — this Mac's section of the record and this Mac's memory: the newer wins, and the retired ids are the union. A Mac whose memory item is gone finds its own section by its hardware. Copies never carry them.
This Mac's memoryA Keychain item per library, no secret, sealed: which key is current, the mode and account last confirmed, the device identity with its hardware marker, a salted hash (on other hardware a new identity is minted), a mirror of the key file and of the family tree, and the second home of this Mac's facts. Deleting it changes nothing on its own: the Mac finds its own section by its hardware. Before unlock it's only a hint and decides nothing.

The new design

Castle — the new design (synced parts marked)

  • On this Mac: Touch ID (or the passphrase) opens the library — and, through the history, every older copy, nothing typed. Entering Castle took everything: the old key, its history, and a Castle anchor from an earlier leave, folded back in (or kept beside the history, unchanged, when the typed secrets couldn't open it).
  • On a new Mac: your passphrase or words; the newest backup's history opens every older one.
  • Sent to another Mac: it lands there under a fresh key of its own; nothing is shared.
  • Lose the passphrase, the words and every Touch ID item: the library and its older copies are gone — said at the door; export a plain copy first if you want one.

Standard — the new design (synced parts marked)

  • On this Mac: the silent item — nothing asked.
  • On a new Mac: your Apple account brings it back, nothing typed: this Mac's own Apple key opens its key file, and, synced, the account's opens the vault's master — when iCloud Keychain carried them.
  • What it means: your Apple account — and anyone with this Mac's login password — can open the library and every copy Variorum made of it since it came into Standard. Out of Castle means not safe, said plainly.
  • After leaving Castle: the library itself opens to them, with everything written in it, in Castle too, and every copy made after the leave. No copy made before the leave does: those open with the passphrase or the twelve words you had on the day you left, through the Castle anchor — leaving never makes new words, so that day's paper keeps working. Your other devices each follow with one secret typed, so their own copies from before open with the words, and with the passphrase when it was the one you typed there to follow; each copy's Opens with line says which, and from which day.
  • If this Mac's own Apple key is lost: an iCloud Keychain reset, or a change of Apple ID: no repair mints a new one. The assessment says the Apple road is lost and offers Re-link's library arm, which mints a fresh own key for this Mac and re-locks the key file under it — and every other Standard key file here that needs one, other libraries' included — re-keying nothing. Local-only libraries get it too. After a change of Apple ID, copies under the old key keep opening through the former account, for as long as its iCloud Keychain keeps the key — this Mac can't delete it there; a stated exception, said on their Opens with line.

Moving between them

The cycle, step by step

The whole cycle as a flow: what you have, what each step does, what’s new, what goes away, and who else can open your library after it. Both crossings make a fresh key, born Castle or Standard for life. Into Castle, everything older comes along; out of Castle, nothing does — what came before goes into the Castle anchor, which only the passphrase and words you had that day open (on a device that followed, the secret typed at its follow and whatever opened that device that day): it keeps the secrets of its day, and every later re-key carries it unchanged. Each pill wears the colour of its key; a struck pill is gone or opens nothing any more.

Leaving or joining an account

Leaving an account (This Mac Only, Stop syncing here, deleting the account from this Mac, Move) re-keys the library, in either mode. The new key's history takes the old one, so your copies keep opening for you, and any anchor rides along unchanged; the account you left opens only the copies from while it synced in Standard — until it replaces its keys or enters Castle — and never what you write after. In Castle: both secrets — the passphrase typed, the words typed or made new. In Standard: this Mac holds the key, so the new key's silent item and this Mac's existing own Apple key carry it with nothing remembered — nothing is minted, as for Replace All Keys, since the account's key never locks the library; the passphrase is typed or set new (Standard's forgot road), and the words typed or made new. The account's Apple key is left alone: the account's other devices still use it. While an anchor exists, a secret made new here says its door's sentence — new words: “Copies from before 12 October still open with the words you're replacing — keep that paper.”; a forgotten passphrase set new: “Copies from before 12 October now open with that day's twelve words — keep that paper.” (Her rulings, 2026-10-13.)

Joining or moving into an account in Castle is entering Castle for this library: the re-key, every Apple key of this Mac's own for the library retired, and the old shared key's checks. Into an account in Standard from Castle is leaving Castle for it: its key change and anchor, with the leave screen's sentence at the consent. Joining an account in the library's own mode — Standard from Standard, or Castle from Castle — adds the master's lock and takes nothing away, so the key stays. When the library's passphrase differs from the account's, enrolling re-locks the library key under the account's; the anchor, if any, is untouched and keeps the secrets of its day. Signing back in to an account the library left is an ordinary turn-on-sync, with its consent.
