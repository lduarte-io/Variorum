Castle key map

What each key is, where it lives, what it's locked with, and what the server holds — today, and in the new design (your rulings of 2026-10-13). A companion to second-opinion.html; the build target alone is castle-target-key-map.html.

The whole idea in two lines. In Castle, your Apple account opens nothing of this library — not the library, not any copy Variorum made of it on this Mac or a device of your account (a library already in Castle before this change: once it retires the old shared Apple key). In Standard, it opens your library and every copy Variorum made of it. In Castle = safe. Out of Castle = not safe.

The cast

Every key is a random 256-bit number except your passphrase and words, which you hold. A locked copy is one key locked with another; the padlock's colour says which key opens it. Nothing on the server — and nothing in a key file — opens anything by itself.

L — the library keyOpens your library file. One per library file; replaced when you enter Castle or replace your keys.
P — your passphraseIn your head. Opens a locked copy of the library key (and, synced, of the master).
W — your twelve wordsOn paper. The same power as the passphrase — your way back if you forget it.
A — an Apple keyLives in iCloud Keychain. Standard's silent road: anyone in your Apple account holds it.
M — the master keyOne per sync account. Opens the sync key and proves to the server that it's you.
C — the sync keyEncrypts everything sent to the server. The server never holds it unlocked.
— a Touch ID itemThis Mac's copy of a key, released only to your finger (Castle). Keychain Access can't read it.
Silent itemThis Mac's copy that opens without asking (Standard). Keychain Access reveals it with the login password.
Locked copy (a padlock)A key locked with another key. Whoever holds the second key gets the first. A key file is a set of these.
This Mac's memoryWhich key is current, the mode, the account, your settings — no secret, and sealed so edits are caught.

How to read the maps

Each map has the same four places: you, this Mac (its Keychain, the library's folder, the backups folder), Apple (iCloud Keychain and iCloud Drive) and the Variorum server. Red outlines are today's gaps; struck-out, dashed chips are keys that don't exist or no longer open anything. The buttons light up what each person can open — You first — and the toggle shows keys as letters or icons.

Today

Standard — this Mac only (today)

  • On this Mac: the silent copy opens the library — nothing asked.
  • On a new Mac: your Apple key (through iCloud Keychain) opens the key file's Apple lock; or your passphrase or words.
  • If everything Apple is lost: your passphrase or twelve words — if you set them.

Standard — synced (today)

  • On this Mac: the silent copy — nothing asked.
  • On a new Mac: sign in to iCloud and Variorum: the Apple key opens the vault's master, nothing typed.
  • The server: holds only locked keys and encrypted writing — never a key that opens them.

Castle — this Mac only (today)

  • On this Mac: Touch ID (or your passphrase every time, with Always ask).
  • On a new Mac: your passphrase or twelve words.
  • The gap: the Apple key is never deleted, so every copy from before Castle still opens through it — and the Mac's login password reveals it in Keychain Access.

Castle — synced (today)

  • On this Mac: Touch ID, or the passphrase.
  • On a new Mac: sign in, then your passphrase or words once.
  • The gap: the same as this-Mac-only: copies from before Castle still open through the Apple key.

“Only this Mac can open your work” (today's exposed state)

  • On this Mac: the silent copy is the only road.
  • If this Mac is lost: nothing opens the library or any backup — not even you. (W-1's root.)

The new design

Castle — the new design (synced parts marked)

  • On this Mac: Touch ID (or the passphrase) opens the library — and, through the history, every older copy, nothing typed.
  • On a new Mac: your passphrase or words; the newest backup's history opens every older one.
  • Lose the passphrase, the words and every Touch ID item: the library and its older copies are gone — said at the door; export a plain copy first if you want one.

Standard — the new design (synced parts marked)

  • On this Mac: the silent item — nothing asked.
  • On a new Mac: your Apple account brings everything back, nothing typed.
  • What it means: your Apple account — and anyone with this Mac's login password — can open the library and every copy Variorum keeps. Out of Castle means not safe, said plainly.

The key history

Moving between them

Entering Castle

  1. You type your passphrase and twelve words (set now if you have none). The screen says plainly: lose them and every Touch ID item, and this library and its older copies are gone — Export a plain copy first is offered. While the store still has the old shared Apple key, its checks run first and the sheet shows what they found (below).
  2. Synced only: a new master M and sync key C are made; your writing on the server is re-encrypted with the new C; the vault is rewritten with no Apple copy.
  3. A new library key L′′ is made and goes into its own item (or nowhere, with Always ask).
  4. The new key file is written: L′′ · locked with P L′′ · locked with W (and L′′ · locked with M if synced), the old key added to the history — no Apple lock. Now any crash still leaves a road in.
  5. The library file is re-encrypted with L′′ — the commit point. The record inside it says Castle; this Mac's memory follows, sealed.
  6. The old key's item, every silent item of the library, the per-library-ID items and the old key files go — each only once the history holds its key.
  7. A fresh backup is made under L′′.
  8. This library's (or the account's) A Apple key is deleted from iCloud Keychain — every device, no recovery — and its id recorded as retired, so a copy of it that comes back is deleted on sight. Synced: each other device asks for your passphrase once, re-encrypts its own library, and deletes and records its own Apple keys too.
  9. Last, the old shared A Apple key is retired, so every older copy's Apple lock is dead, wherever the copy is. (There is no keep-for-now: to deal with something the sheet listed first, cancel and come back.)

Leaving Castle

  1. You confirm with Touch ID or the passphrase, after the plain warning: your Apple account — and anyone who can sign in to this Mac — will be able to open this library and every copy Variorum kept, including copies made while you were in Castle.
  2. A fresh A Apple key is made, with a new id. Only this device makes it; your other devices wait for it.
  3. The key file gains L · locked with A; synced, the vault gains M · locked with A.
  4. L moves from its item to a silent item; the record and this Mac's memory say Standard.
  5. Your other devices follow without asking anything; each adds its own Apple lock when the new key arrives.

Another device follows

  1. Another of your devices entered Castle or replaced the keys; this device sees the vault changed.
  2. It asks for your passphrase or words — once. Typing the old passphrase after a change elsewhere is refused with the change named: “Your passphrase was changed on [device] on [day] — enter the new one.”
  3. It gets the new M and C, re-encrypts its own library with a new key (the old key joins its history), and — into Castle — deletes its own Apple keys and records them as retired.
  4. Until then it still opens and you can write; it just doesn't sync, and a banner says why. A follow into Standard asks nothing.

Opening an older backup

Today (VX-209). The backup's own Apple lock opens it silently; this Mac's one Keychain slot for the library is overwritten with the backup's key; the Touch ID copy is deleted; the library is now Standard. Bringing your Castle library back finds the silent slot and stays Standard.
New design.
  1. Inside Variorum, you pick an older backup of this library (or one is found at its place, say after a Time Machine rollback — Touch ID or the passphrase comes first either way).
  2. Its key is found through the history — today's key opens the older ones — so nothing more is typed.
  3. The copy is re-encrypted with today's key, straight into place; the library you had joins your older libraries.
  4. Your library is back as it was then, in the mode it's in now. A Castle library stays in Castle.
