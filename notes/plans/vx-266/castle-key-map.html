Castle key map

What each key is, where it lives, what it's locked with, and what the server holds — today, and in the new design (your rulings of 2026-10-13). A companion to second-opinion.html; the build target alone is castle-target-key-map.html.

The whole idea in two lines. In Castle, your Apple account opens nothing of this library — not the library, not any copy Variorum made of it on this Mac or a device of your account (the stated exceptions aside: a library already in Castle before this change, until it retires the old shared Apple key; copies from while a library synced with an account in Standard; copies under a Standard key shared with another Mac by Send Library). In Standard, it opens your library and every copy Variorum made of it outside Castle; what was written in Castle stays Castle's. In Castle = safe. Out of Castle = not safe.

The cast

Every key is a random 256-bit number except your passphrase and words, which you hold. A locked copy is one key locked with another; the padlock's colour says which key opens it. Nothing on the server — and nothing in a key file — opens anything by itself.

L — the library keyOpens your library file. One per library file; replaced when you enter Castle or replace your keys.
P — your passphraseIn your head. Opens a locked copy of the library key (and, synced, of the master).
W — your twelve wordsOn paper. The same power as the passphrase — your way back if you forget it.
A — an Apple keyLives in iCloud Keychain. Standard's silent road: anyone in your Apple account holds it.
M — the master keyOne per sync account. Opens the sync key and proves to the server that it's you.
C — the sync keyEncrypts everything sent to the server. The server never holds it unlocked.
— a Touch ID itemThis Mac's copy of a key, released only to your finger (Castle). Keychain Access can't read it.
Silent itemThis Mac's copy that opens without asking (Standard). Keychain Access reveals it with the login password.
Locked copy (a padlock)A key locked with another key. Whoever holds the second key gets the first. A key file is a set of these.
This Mac's memoryWhich key is current, the mode, the account, your settings — no secret, and sealed so edits are caught.

Red outlines are today's gaps; struck-out, dashed chips are keys that don't exist or no longer open anything. The buttons light up what each person can open — You first — and the toggle shows keys as letters or icons.

The new design

Castle — the new design (synced parts marked)

  • On this Mac: Touch ID (or the passphrase) opens the library — and, through the history, every older copy, nothing typed.
  • On a new Mac: your passphrase or words; the newest backup's history opens every older one.
  • Lose the passphrase, the words and every Touch ID item: the library and its older copies are gone — said at the door; export a plain copy first if you want one.

Standard — the new design (synced parts marked)

  • On this Mac: the silent item — nothing asked.
  • On a new Mac: your Apple account brings everything back, nothing typed.
  • What it means: your Apple account — and anyone with this Mac's login password — can open the library and every copy Variorum keeps from outside Castle. Out of Castle means not safe, said plainly; what was written in Castle stays Castle's.

The key history

Moving between them

Entering Castle

  1. You type your passphrase and twelve words (set now if you have none). The screen says plainly: lose them and every Touch ID item, and this library and its older copies are gone — Export a plain copy first is offered. While the store still has the old shared Apple key, its checks run first and the sheet shows what they found (below).
  2. Synced only: a new master M and sync key C are made; your writing on the server is re-encrypted with the new C; the vault is rewritten with no Apple copy.
  3. A new library key L′′ is made and goes into its own item, made at this press (or nowhere, with Always ask).
  4. The new key file is written: L′′ · locked with P L′′ · locked with W (and L′′ · locked with M if synced), the old key added to the history — no Apple lock. Now any crash still leaves a road in.
  5. The record is written into the staged copy, saying Castle; then the library file is swapped to L′′ — the commit point. Copies pause from the new key's making to the swap, so every copy carries a matching key and record. This Mac's memory follows.
  6. The old key's item, every silent item of the library, the per-library-ID items, the escrow items and the old key files go — each only once the history holds its key.
  7. A fresh backup is made under L′′.
  8. The A Apple keys this library alone uses — its own and every device-own key — are deleted from iCloud Keychain, every device, no recovery, and their ids recorded as retired, so a copy that comes back is deleted on sight. The account's goes when the account itself enters Castle: its rotation removes the vault's Apple copy. Synced: each other device asks for your passphrase once, re-encrypts its own library, and records and deletes its own Apple keys too.
  9. Last, the old shared A Apple key is retired, so every older copy's Apple lock is dead, wherever the copy is. (There is no keep-for-now: to deal with something the sheet listed first, cancel and come back.)

Leaving Castle

  1. You type your passphrase and your twelve words — leaving Castle is a key change, like entering it — after the plain warning: from now on your Apple account, and anyone who can sign in to this Mac, can open this library and its copies from before you entered Castle. Copies made while you were in Castle keep opening only with your passphrase or words.
  2. Synced only: a new master M″″ and sync key C″″; your writing on the server re-encrypted; the vault rewritten with the new master under your passphrase, your words and a fresh account A Apple key.
  3. A new library key L″″ goes into a silent item; a fresh A Apple key is made with a new id — only this device makes it.
  4. The new key file: L″″ · locked with A L″″ · locked with P L″″ · locked with W (and the new master, synced). Its history takes back the keys from before Castle — never the keys made in Castle. History flows into Castle, never out.
  5. The record, now saying Standard, goes into the staged copy; the library file is swapped to L″″. Copies pause from the new key to the swap.
  6. Castle's items go; a fresh backup is made under L″″.
  7. Your other devices follow without asking anything once the new Apple key arrives; each re-keys its own library the same way, leaving its Castle keys behind.

Another device follows

  1. Another of your devices entered Castle or replaced the keys; this device sees the vault changed.
  2. It asks for your passphrase or words — once. Typing the old passphrase after a change elsewhere is refused with the change named: “Your passphrase was changed on [device] on [day] — enter the new one.”
  3. It gets the new M and C, re-encrypts its own library with a new key (the old key joins its history), and — into Castle — records every Apple key its key file names as retired (the account's, its own, the old shared one) and deletes its own.
  4. Until then it still opens and you can write; it just doesn't sync, and a banner says why. A follow into Standard asks nothing, and ends with a notice: your Apple account can now open this library and every copy Variorum kept of it, including copies made while you were in Castle.

Opening an older backup

Today (VX-209). The backup's own Apple lock opens it silently; this Mac's one Keychain slot for the library is overwritten with the backup's key; the Touch ID copy is deleted; the library is now Standard. Bringing your Castle library back finds the silent slot and stays Standard.
New design.
  1. Inside Variorum, you pick an older backup of this library (or one is found at its place, say after a Time Machine rollback). Whatever unlocks your library comes first either way: Touch ID or the passphrase in Castle, the silent item in Standard.
  2. Its key is found through the history — today's key opens the older ones — so nothing more is typed.
  3. The copy is re-encrypted with today's key, straight into place; the library you had joins your older libraries.
  4. Your library is back as it was then, in the mode it's in now. A Castle library stays in Castle.
