Castle lifecycle

One person, one library, the ordinary road — what is written at each stage, and every way the library and its backups can be opened, depending on the choices you make. The contract behind every line is notes/CastleRules.md; the rule numbers in brackets point at it. This page is the target: the corrector phase proves each stage with a test, then regenerates this page from the run so it can never drift.

Two facts hold on every row below. A quiet road opens only a key it directly holds — it never reaches through the key file's list of earlier keys; only typed secrets do that [4]. And the Apple road, being your iCloud Keychain, also works on your other Macs signed into the same Apple account [8].

The stages

1 · You create a library Standard

What happens
A random key is made. The key file is written beside the library, with the key locked under your twelve words and under this Mac's quiet roads. The words are shown once: "write these down now". A sealed copy of the key file goes into this Mac's keychain. A plain random device ID is written [1, 2, 23].

4 · You change your passphrase, or make new words Standard

What happens
The current key is re-locked under the new secret; the key itself does not change. Backup A's own key file still wants the old secret — and your new secret opens it too, because it is the same key [6].

5 · You change your Apple ID, or iCloud Keychain is reset Standard — "the real issue"

What happens
The Apple road may stop working. The app can't see this coming and doesn't try to. At the next open it uses the on-device road — which never depended on iCloud — and quietly makes a fresh Apple road under the new account. Nothing is asked. Only if both quiet roads are gone (a keychain reset) does it ask for your passphrase or words first [8].

Backups so far
Open with their day's secrets, today's, or the on-device road [4, 5]. Their old Apple lock is dead, and that is fine.

The reminder of rule 3 still shows once here if you have no passphrase. This is why there is no secretless library: the one state where an Apple ID change could strand you no longer exists [2, 25].

6 · Use Castle Castle — Touch ID or Always ask

What happens
You type your passphrase. A new Castle key is made; every earlier key moves into its history, under the new key [4, 11]. The Apple keychain entry is deleted. If you chose Touch ID, this Mac keeps the key behind your fingerprint; under Always ask, no copy stays on the device. The scheduled plain-text export is turned off, with one notice. Synced: the account gets a new sync key in one write and keeps the old one in its list; every other device must type a secret once to follow [10, 15].

You type your passphrase. A fresh Standard key with an empty history and a fresh Apple road [12]. Your text, the server's rows and the sync key are Standard from now on — leaving is your choice about your writing. This Mac's Touch ID item and its sealed key-file copy now hold the new key. The only thing that stays Castle-only after leaving is a backup you made while in Castle [13]. Anything else from before the leave opens with the secrets of its day — today's new Standard secrets reach nothing older than the leave. The leave screen says: keep your Castle passphrase and words — your Castle backups will always need them.

9 · You open a Castle-era backup after leaving Look inside, or Make this my library

Look inside
Type backup B's Castle passphrase or words. It opens read-only. Nothing is written — no key file, no keychain item, no change to your library. Close it and the app forgets it [17].
Make this my library
One confirmed door. Backup B becomes a new library with a fresh key in the posture you are in now (Standard, here), under your current pair, and your current library steps aside whole into Previous Libraries [17]. Backup B itself is untouched and still Castle-only. The app's Restore from Backup button is this door.

10 · Someone AirDrops you their library any posture

Look inside
Opens with their secrets, read-only, writes nothing [17].
Make this my library
It becomes a new library of yours, with a fresh identity and a fresh key in your posture, under your current pair; you never leave Castle by opening something [17, 18]. From here the two copies diverge — sharing a library across devices is what sync is for.

11 · You move to a new Mac with Migration Assistant any posture

What happens
The library and its key file move with you. This Mac's keychain items may or may not arrive — that is Apple's behaviour, which the app doesn't answer for [25]. If the quiet roads didn't survive the move, the first open asks for your passphrase or words and re-makes them [8]; Castle re-arms Touch ID on the new hardware the same way. Nothing is "taken over" and nothing is fingerprinted — the device ID is a plain random value [23].

12 · You turn on sync, or join from a new device any posture

Turning sync on first
Your library's passphrase and twelve words become the account's. Nothing is replaced [7].
Joining from another device
You type the account's passphrase or words once. Your library's own pair is replaced by the account's, and the app tells you so and shows the words: write them down; the old ones still open backups you made before today (same key, old locks) [7, 14]. For anyone who never wrote their words down, this is the reminder, while they still have access.
Castle, account-wide
When one device enters Castle, each other device types a secret once at its next sync to follow; until then it reads only what it already has, through its own quiet road [10]. The account keeps every sync key it has had, so a device joining later reads everything [15].
Leaving the account
Changes none of your keys or secrets; your library just stops syncing. The server keeps what you pushed. Signing back in is a join [16].

13 · A crash in the middle of any of this any posture

What happens
Every act that changes keys is planned. The next open finds either the old state or the new one, finishes or rolls back from what is on disk, and says which [20]. A key is deleted only after its replacement has been verified [22].

14 · Something is wrong on disk any posture

What happens
This Mac remembers four things about its library: its ID, its current key, its device ID and a sealed copy of its key file — and only the app's own doors change them [21]. If the file in the live place isn't the one it remembers — a swapped-in file, a replayed old file, or a backup pair you copied in by hand — the app refuses to open, says what it found, and offers the two doors: Look inside, or Make this my library. It never repairs by guessing. The honest way to go back to a backup is the app's own Restore from Backup door (stage 9).
Lost the key file?
The app kept copies: beside the library, inside every backup, sealed in this Mac's keychain [1]. Synced libraries recover from the account with the twelve words [27].

What we don't promise

  • That Apple's roads keep working across Apple ID changes, sign-outs, keychain resets or a move to a new Mac. We promise you never need them [25].
  • Anything about stores from before this design. Export or sign in to sync before the switch, then start fresh [26].
  • That a device which hasn't followed into Castle can't read its own local copy. It can, through its own quiet road, until it follows [10].
  • Recovery of a library whose every key-file copy and every backup are gone [27].
