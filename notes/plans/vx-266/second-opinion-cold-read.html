Cold read of second-opinion.md (VX-266)

Reviewer: second cold reviewer, read-only. Target: notes/plans/vx-266/second-opinion.md. Context read: design-proposal.md, cold-read-1.md, second-opinion-independent-notes.md, pre-13x-postures-design.md §1–§7/§14, and the code at every point the second opinion cites (StoreProtection, AppBootstrap, StoreLaunchGate, StoreKeyEnvelope, StoreIdentity, AppleKeyCustody, ProtectionController, StoreBackup, BackupRetention, ICloudDriveOffer, LibraryAdoption, LibraryOpenCopy, RestoreHoldModel, StoreLockView, CorpusRotation). Worktree at f91f7205.

One paragraph. The second opinion's four changes and its structural option are, in the main, well reasoned and its code citations are accurate where I could trace them. Its sharpest contribution — that a library this Mac knows to be in Castle still opens an older copy through the Apple account first (§2.2), that plans beside the library are forgeable (§2.3), and that two Macs sharing one iCloud Drive backup folder prune and offer to delete each other's backups (§2.4) — all check out against the code. What is wrong is in the framing and reach of the two biggest moves. §3 (retire the Apple key) is written as if it helps the author, but by its own cost 4 it does not: her stores and every current beta tester keep today's shared key and get none of §3's benefits (M2). §3 also calls the follower-mint fix "one change," when in fact deleting A at entering Castle re-creates the exact premature-mint / orphaned-vault-copy concurrency hazard that today's "never delete A" rule exists to avoid, reopening the settled F2/F3 ladder (M1). §2.1 (drop the history key) deliberately re-accepts cold read 1's only BLOCKER (B1) and understates its blast radius as "one narrow case after you leave Castle," when it also widens Apple reach to retired/exposed-era keys in Standard (M3). MAJOR

M1 · §3 / cost 3 · "today's minting guards need that one change" understates a reopened concurrency hazard that §3 itself creates

What's wrong. §3 deletes the account's Apple key A at entering Castle and mints a fresh one at leaving. Cost 3 says only: "Followers must wait for the acting device's key, never mint their own; today's minting guards need that one change." That is not one change, and the need for it is a hazard §3 manufactures.

Evidence / mechanism (traced). Today A is never deleted (AppleKeyCustody.swift:189-191; the key is per-iCloud-account and shared — account(scope:) at :135 keys it by scope only). reconcileAppleKey's mint gate is mintAllowed = vaultAppleKeyID == nil || holdsMaster (ProtectionController.swift:1636-1638), and a follower into Standard holds the master. The only reason a follower does not mint a second A today is that read() returns the still-present shared A (:1726 runs readOrMint only after read() answered nil; :1659-1663), so minted is false and nothing new is created. Under §3, entering Castle deletes A, so on the leave that mints the fresh A, a follower that reconciles before the fresh A has propagated reads nil, holdsMaster is true ⇒ mintAllowed true ⇒ it mints its own A₂. iCloud Keychain then keeps one of the two and discards the other (the proposal's own m1 fact), and if A₂ wins, the vault's M-under-A_fresh copy is orphaned under a key no device holds — the precise F2 (MAJOR-3) failure the two-round pre-13x ladder was built to prevent (ProtectionController.swift:1600-1668, the F2/F3 commentary). To stop it, appleKeyWaiting (today fired only when !holdsMaster, :1649-1653) must now fire for a master-holding follower during a leave, which needs a new "am I the leave-Castle actor" signal threaded into the reconciler — not present today.

Why it matters. The author is being asked to say yes to D3 believing the custody rework is "one change." It is a re-opening of settled, previously-buggy concurrency logic (VX-99 was "two Apple-copy deciders"), and §3 is what introduces the window by abandoning the invariant (never delete A) that currently closes it.

Fix. State that D3 requires rebuilding reconcileAppleKey's mint/wait gate so a follower never mints during a leave (wait for the acting device's named A), and name this as reopening F2/F3 with its own convergence pins — not "one change."

M2 · §3 / "What it buys" vs cost 4 · §3's headline benefits do not reach the author's own stores or any current user

What's wrong. The short version and §3's "What it buys" are written unconditionally: "every copy Variorum ever made of that library … stops opening through the Apple account at that moment," "§2.2's attack dies," "two plain rules replace the offer." But cost 4 says: "Existing stores keep today's shared key for their older copies. The migration can't safely delete a key other libraries or accounts may use. For your devices and the few beta testers, the offer stays as it is today; everyone who installs after launch starts on per-library keys."

Mechanism. Today's A is one shared item per iCloud account (AppleKeyCustody.swift:135, account(scope:) = "<scope>-apple-key" — no library/account discriminator), used by every library and Variorum account on that iCloud. The migration cannot delete it without stranding the other users of it, so every store that exists today — the author's Pro and Air, every 0.9.x install — keeps the shared, never-deleted key and the existing "delete your backups?" offer. §3 changes only libraries first created on a post-launch build.

Why it matters. VX-266 is a pre-launch pass on the author's Castle. D3 is recommended "yes," but for the reader of this document it is inert: entering Castle on her store will still not cover her older copies, and §2.2's attack still lives on her store's Apple road. The decision table (D3) and the short version give no hint of this; only cost 4, buried in §3, does.

Fix. Move cost 4's limit into the short version and D3's recommendation line: "§3 changes new installs only; your own stores keep today's shared key and the offer." Consider (and price) whether the migration could make a store whose shared A no other library/account uses eligible — otherwise D3 is a decision about future users, not about this launch.

M3 · §2.1 · dropping the history key re-accepts cold read 1's only BLOCKER (B1), and "one narrow case after you leave Castle" understates its reach

What's wrong. Cold read 1's single BLOCKER, B1, was "after leaving Castle the Apple account reaches Castle-era retired keys"; the proposal fixed it by wrapping the history key H under the passphrase and words only (never A, never the master — design-proposal.md §3.2). §2.1 drops H and wraps the history under the library's current key. In Standard the current key has an Apple copy, so A → current key → history → every earlier key, which is B1's outcome. §2.1 characterizes the cost as "one narrow case after you leave Castle."

Mechanism (traced against the proposal). (a) After leaving Castle, the live key file gains apple for the current key; the history under it then exposes keys retired while in Castle — B1 exactly, and the second opinion admits this in the same section. (b) The characterization "one narrow case" is too narrow: §2.1 also changes Standard-mode reach. The proposal keeps H off the device in Standard, so an older-key backup there needs the passphrase/words (design-proposal.md §3.2). Under §2.1, A reaches the whole history in Standard — so a Standard Replace All Keys no longer keeps the retired key out of the Apple account, and exposed-era backups folded into the history become Apple-openable. That is broader than "after you leave Castle," even if it is consistent with decision 30 ("in Standard no rotation shuts out an Apple-account holder").

Why it matters. §2.1's own argument that H "protects nothing at all" for users who never re-key is fair, but the recommendation steers the author to re-accept the reviewed BLOCKER under a "simplification" banner while pricing the security delta as narrower than it is. The author should weigh this as "re-accept B1 and change Standard's retired-key exposure," with the honest leave sentence, not as a free simplification.

Fix. Re-state §2.1's security delta as two cases — (1) Castle-era keys reachable via A after leaving Castle (B1 re-accepted); (2) retired/exposed-era keys reachable via A in Standard — and tie both to decision 30 so the author sees the full picture. (The rest of §2.1 is sound; see the clean bill.)

MINOR

m1 · §2.2 · the attack is stated as general to "a library this Mac knows is in Castle" but only lands for a LOCAL-ONLY Castle library

What's wrong. §2.2 (and the short version item 1) presents the login-password file-swap as working for any Castle library. For a synced Castle library it does not: the existing route logic blocks it.

Mechanism (traced). At the route, effectiveGate computes enrolled = syncCustodyPresent(scope, storeID) (StoreLaunchGate.swift:408); the planted backup carries the same storeID, and the Mac still holds the sync custody item for it, so enrolled = true. Posture.derive then returns .indeterminate(shape:) (StoreProtection.swift:91-93), and shapeReading reads rawKeyCustody, which is .gatedOrNone under Castle (no silent item; StoreLaunchGate.swift:433-437) ⇒ Castle ⇒ effectiveGate = .biometric ⇒ the Castle lobby, Touch ID required. Only a local-only Castle library has enrolled = false, so localReading sees the planted apple copy and returns .standard (StoreProtection.swift:101-109) ⇒ .auto ⇒ silent open via StoreLobby.openWithAppleKey (AppBootstrap.swift:350-357), re-landed at :548-556. The trace the second opinion gives is accurate for that case — and VX-209's appendix case is local-only — but the section's title and the short version overclaim.

Fix. Scope §2.2's threat to local-only Castle libraries (and older copies under the shared key / another account's key, which §2.2's last paragraph already notes), and credit that the synced case is already defended by enrolled ⇒ indeterminate ⇒ Castle shape.

m2 · §2.3 "Related, and smaller" · moving re-key-in-progress into the key file trusts an unsealed, forgeable field pre-unlock — against §2.3's own premise

What's wrong. §2.3's whole thesis is that files beside the library are forgeable by the login-password holder, so plans must be sealed. Its closing suggestion — "let the key file carry a re-key in progress itself … the lobby then decides from one file … and never needs to read plans" — puts trust in the key file, which is exactly the unsealed, attacker-writable artifact the section distrusts (and which the author's own independent notes, point 8, flag: "the route decides from the plaintext key file … nothing pre-unlock is authenticated").

Mechanism. StoreKeyEnvelope is plain JSON (StoreKeyEnvelope.load decodes it with no authentication, :501-519); an attacker who can write the container can set a false "re-key in progress (current + pending)". The lobby is read-only (it only chooses which road to offer, and the real secret/key is still required to open), so this is not a silent downgrade — but it is internally inconsistent to seal the plans because the key file is forgeable and then ask the lobby to trust a new forgeable field in that same key file.

Fix. Drop the "smaller" suggestion, or state that the re-key-in-progress field is a road hint only, never relied on for anything the key cannot already prove, and keep the plan (sealed) as the authority.

m3 · §2.3 · "seal with a key derived from the library's current key" is underspecified for the re-key plans and Always-ask devices

What's wrong. The plans most needing tamper-proofing are the ones that change the current key (enter Castle, Replace All Keys, leave Castle). "Seal under the current key" is ambiguous when the plan's purpose is to retire that key: at a crash mid-re-key, is the seal under the old key L (write time) or the new key L′? §2.3 says "checked after unlock, when the key is in hand," but mid-re-key the key in hand may be either L or L′ depending on where the crash fell (the VX-80 window — AppBootstrap.swift:498-556 reasons about exactly this).

Mechanism. On an Always-ask device there is no key at rest (no silent or Touch ID item), so the seal can be verified only after the user types a secret at the next launch — which is acceptable (the attacker also cannot seal), but it means a resumed plan cannot be verified silently, contrary to the "resumed at your next Touch ID unlock" framing the attack assumes.

Fix. Specify that a re-key plan is sealed under a key the resume can always re-derive after unlock (e.g. the old key, recoverable through the history once any current road unlocks), and that Always-ask resumes verify after the typed unlock.

m4 · §2.4 · the cross-Mac PRUNING hazard is sound; the cross-Mac OFFER-listing is framed as a bug but may be correct for a synced library

What's wrong. The pruning hazard is real and well traced: existingBackups (StoreBackup.swift:704-716) lists every app-named snapshot regardless of library or Mac, prune (:609-615) and BackupRetention.keep (BackupRetention.swift:87-118) count them all, and the iCloud Drive offer opens the picker at driveRoot (ICloudDriveOffer.swift:101-106) with backups landing at <pick>/Variorum/Backups (BackupDestination.backupsFolder), so two Macs defaulting the picker share one folder and prune each other's history — a genuine data-loss hazard. But §2.4 also lists "the Castle offer on one Mac lists the other Mac's backups for deletion" as a hazard; for a synced library those are Standard-era copies of the same account's writing, so offering them for deletion to make Castle cover everything is arguably the correct behavior, not a defect.

Fix. Keep the pruning hazard (unambiguous). Re-cast the offer-listing as "cross-Mac visibility" and note it is correct for a synced library and only surprising for a local-only one. (The proposed per-library-and-Mac attribution is the right fix for pruning regardless.)

m5 · §2.5 · reopens cold read 1's m9 (device-identity migration leg), which the proposal deliberately resolved by withdrawal

What's wrong. §2.5 proposes moving the device identity out of the sync-keys item into "what this device remembers." Cold read 1 raised exactly this (m9) and the proposal resolved it by withdrawal — "the device identity stays in the sync-keys item … no leg needed" (cold-read-1.md dispositions, m9). §2.5 reintroduces the migration leg (a new identity mints a new device_cursor row, the 13j purge-horizon drop-out / returner, corners 37) that the withdrawal avoided.

Fix. §2.5 is explicitly optional ("Worth doing if the open-road phase has room; not required"), so this is only a flag: note that it reopens m9 and carries m9's migration-leg cost, so "optional simplification" is not free.

Clean bill — load-bearing claims I checked and found sound

  1. §2.1's four "half-locked" states of the proposal's history key H are accurate. A passphrase change leaves a following device's local H under the old secret with no vault road (H is never in the vault — proposal §3.2), so it strands until the words are typed; Standard keeps no H on the device (every older-key restore asks); H is minted only when a secret is typed (migration must ask — proposal §7 step 5). All confirmed against design-proposal.md §3.2/§7.
  2. §2.1's "entering Castle is unaffected" is sound. The re-keyed current key L′ carries no apple copy (StoreKeyEnvelope.setAppleWrappedCopy is never called under Castle), so the history under L′ is not Apple-reachable; nothing written after the switch is reachable through A. Matches cold read 1's clean bill 3.
  3. §2.1's alternative genuinely removes the passphrase-change strand. With the history under the library key L, a follower holds L and opens its past with nothing typed (Appendix A's "passphrase changed on the Air, then a key replacement on the Pro" is correct).
  4. §2.2's attack is real for a local-only Castle library. Traced end to end: StoreProtection.swift:101-109 → .standard; AppBootstrap.swift:350-357 opens via the Apple key; :548-556 re-lands it silently; and the proposal (§4, "Someone puts an older, Apple-openable copy where your library was") opens-through-Apple-then-reconciles exactly as §2.2 charges.
  5. §2.3's planted-plan attack is real against the proposal as written. The proposal's only guard is "a plan whose world doesn't match is ignored as stale," and the world is the library ID + key fingerprints, which are public in every key file (StoreKeyEnvelope), so a login-password holder can plant a world-matching plan. No seal exists in the proposal.
  6. §2.4's cross-Mac pruning hazard is real and correctly traced (existingBackups:704, prune:609, BackupRetention:87-118, ICloudDriveOffer.driveRoot:101-106, backupsFolder), and the proposal/research do not name it (research C11 notes iCloud Drive placement but not the two-Mac shared-folder prune).
  7. §2.4's six "other hazards" trace to the cited code: ProtectionController.ensuredEnvelope:1129-1142 mints a new store ID on a foreign key file; LibraryAdoption:295-304 lands the store before the key file with best-effort try? writes; LibraryOpenCopy.swift:114 shows "your current passphrase (online)" from a mere credentials.master flag; RestoreHoldModel.swift:85-89 ignores clear()'s failure return (LibraryRestoreHold.clear returns false on a failed removeItem, :86-90) and clears the banner anyway; .stale-* key files are invisible to keyFileURLs (StoreKeyEnvelope:464-475).
  8. §2.2's fix is not bypassed by a container delete on macOS. Cold read 1's m19 establishes the device Keychain survives container deletion on macOS; since the never-lower rule is keyed by library ID through the Keychain-resident remembered record, an attacker who reuses the old library ID still trips it — the surviving item helps the rule rather than defeating it.
  9. The agreement table's core moves (copies re-encrypted under the current key; one decider/one writer; the record local-only / no lockstep; per-key custody) are consistent with the proposal and with cold read 1's clean bill; I found no internal contradiction among §2.1–§2.5 beyond m2's key-file/plan tension.

Apple-platform facts I did not independently verify (no web access): iCloud Keychain deletion propagation and same-key conflict resolution (§3 costs 2–3), and Keychain survival across app/container deletion (§2.2/§2.5). The second opinion already marks these APPLE-FACTS-PENDING; I assume cold read 1's recorded findings (one survivor by UUID; macOS data-protection keychain outside the container) and flag them UNVERIFIED here.
