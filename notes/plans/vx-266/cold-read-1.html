The read in one paragraph. The diagnosis is accurate: every mechanism it rests on is in the code where it says (clean bill 1), and its five causes are a fair reading of the tickets. The structural moves that answer those causes — Keychain items per key, one decider with no writing reads, every copy brought under the library's current key, plans outside the library file, one catalog and one route finder — are sound and fix VX-209/VX-228/W-1 at the root. The two genuinely NEW mechanisms are where the trouble is: the history key wrapped under the account master re-opens Castle-era keys to the Apple account once a library leaves Castle (B1), and the in-file record with its change counter lets a copy decide the library's mode and key — non-causally between two Macs, and forgeably by Castle's own adversary (M1). Both can be dropped or narrowed without losing a ticket (m16). The account model needs to reach row ownership (M2), the migration needs a real road back and an answer for every old shape (M3), two Touch ID rules weaken protection or destroy it transiently (M4, M7), VX-212's answer deletes user rows (M6), and the phase is several phases (M5).

Findings (severity-ranked)

BLOCKER

B1 · §3.2 (the history key), §12 ("Wrap the history under the current library key… set aside"), §3.8 promises 2/8 · the history key is wrapped under the account master, so in Standard it IS reachable through the Apple account — leaving Castle (or any road into a Standard account) hands every Castle-era retired key to an Apple-account holder.

  • What's wrong. §3.2 puts the history key H "wrapped in the key file under the passphrase, the words and the account master — never the Apple key", and argues: "Because it is never wrapped under the Apple key, leaving Castle later never makes keys that were only ever Castle-protected reachable through the Apple account." §12 rejects the simpler design for exactly this reason. But H is not replaced at Leave Castle (§3.2: replaced "at every re-key"; leaving Castle is not one — decision 7), and Leave Castle adds the master under A to the vault.
  • Evidence (mechanism). PostureSwitchRunner.leaveCastle writes fetched.format.settingAppleHeldCopy(masterKey: material.masterSymmetricKey, appleKey: …) into the vault (App/Variorum/Shared/PostureSwitch.swift:1452-1463); §3.10 keeps that protocol unchanged. Sequence: synced library, Standard (L0) → Use Castle (L1, H1; M1 has no A copy) → Replace All Keys in Castle, or a follow of one from another device (L2, H2 under P/W/M2; history {L0, L1}) → backups under L1 sit in the second folder (iCloud Drive, decision 24) → Use Standard (vault gains M2 under A; H2 untouched). Now someone fully in the Apple account (A from iCloud Keychain; a session via an account-password reset by the iCloud email — pre-13x §3.4's own "who can obtain it" column) fetches the vault → unwraps M2 with A → unwraps H2 from any post-switch pair's key file (pairs carry "the whole key file of its day, history included", §3.2) → L1 → opens every Castle-era backup made under L1. Today those pairs carry L1 only under P1/W1/M1 (M1 retired by the rotation), so they stay Castle-protected after leaving (pre-13x §3.4's Castle → Standard row: "backups: untouched"). The same holds for a Castle-born library joining a Standard account (joinStandard → the follow into Standard) and for "Move it to y@" into a Standard account (§3.7). Locally-only libraries are not affected (no M wrap).
  • Why the proof misses it. Promise 2 checks only libraries in Castle; promise 8 only the entering direction. No promise checks "keys retired while in Castle stay unreachable through the Apple account after the library leaves", so the scenario test would pass this design.
  • Proposed fix. Never wrap H under M (P, W and the device item only): what the M wrap adds over the local P/W copies is the vault road for a word changed on another device (the old word's local copy still opens it) and — in Standard — the Apple road. If the M wrap is wanted for Castle's vault road, drop it (and re-mint H, which needs P and W typed) at every road into Standard. Add a promise — "after leaving Castle, no key retired while in Castle opens through the Apple account" — and a fixed sequence for it.

MAJOR

M1 · §3.2 (the change counter), §3.6 (the open rule's "newer change counter" arm), §4 "Two Macs passing…", §11 ("Newest choice wins") · the counter cannot say which choice is newer, the record it lives in is forgeable by the very adversary Castle stops, and "the copy's key wins" lets a copy choose the library's future key.

  • (a) Not causal. Each device increments its own counter at its own acts, so two Macs holding copies of one local-only library diverge, and "higher" means "toggled more often", not "chosen later". Sequence: the Pro sends library X to the Air (both remember counter 1, Standard). The Air tries Castle and leaves it (Air: 3, Standard). Later the Pro enters Castle (Pro: 2, Castle — her latest choice). The Air's copy reaches the Pro (hand-off, or a shared second backup folder): 3 > 2, so by §3.6 "the copy's mode, account and key win" — the Pro's library drops to Standard under the Air's Apple-wrapped key, and the confirm calls an older choice "a newer choice made on another Mac". This breaks rule 1 ("nothing else changes it") and contradicts promise 4 ("never at a restore, an open").
  • (b) Forgeable. §3.2: the record is "protected for free — only the library's key can read or change it". For every Standard-era copy that key is Apple-reachable, and an Apple-account holder (Castle's named adversary, pre-13x §2) can also mint a key and wrap it under A. The library ID is not secret (it is in every key file, and those sit in the iCloud Drive second folder). So a crafted pair — the victim's library ID, a key the attacker knows wrapped under A, a record saying Castle, counter 999 — placed in the second folder (which §3.6's catalog newly lists in the restore list) opens through the Apple road without a prompt (§3.4 road 3), wins by counter, and installs the attacker's key as the library's current key while the confirm reports Castle ("the confirm says which and why", §4). The only backstop is §3.3's "if an Apple wrap is ever found, the repair is a re-key" — a re-key needs P and W typed (decision 33), so it is an act that can wait, and writing done before it lands under the attacker's key. A variant without the Apple wrap uses a Standard-era key the attacker extracted earlier and that sits in the victim's own history (§3.6's route finder opens it through H); once the Apple-openable pairs are gone — exactly what the recommended "Bring them into Castle" achieves — no Apple wrap of that key is ever "found".
  • (c) Undefined pieces. On an exact tie "the safer mode wins" but not whose key (a Castle win over this device's Standard key needs a re-key: that key may be Apple-wrapped in older pairs); "this device's previous key moves into the history" needs the WINNING copy's history key, which an Apple-road open never has (H is never under A).
  • Proposed fix. A copy never supplies the library's key and never lowers its protection: every copy of the same library is re-encrypted under the library's current key (or a freshly minted one when raising to Castle), and a copy's recorded mode can at most RAISE protection — or be offered as a question — never lower it. With that rule the counter (and arguably the in-file record) is unnecessary for local-only libraries; synced ones already defer to the vault.

M2 · §3.7 (the account table; "Rows are stamped with an account when the library joins it, not at every sign-in") · the account model is disconnected from row ownership, and the stated change would wedge sync.

  • Evidence. New rows take their owner from the SESSION at mint: Operations.currentOwnerID() → ownerIdentity.current() (Sources/VariorumCore/Operations/Operations.swift:38; mint sites e.g. Operations+Container.swift:308/359/375, Operations+Comments.swift:37). Rows minted signed out are born unowned, and the sign-in pass exists because "an unowned row would be refused by the push guard, and the sign-in flow runs the adoption pass before syncing for exactly that reason" (Sources/VariorumCore/Operations/OwnerIdentity.swift:12-17; AppServices.runOwnerAdoption, "at sign-in and at every signed-in launch").
  • Mechanisms. (1) Stop the per-sign-in stamp as written ⇒ a library that belongs to X, edited while signed out, carries unowned rows when she signs back in as X (row 2 of the table: "In sync") ⇒ the push refuses them. (2) Row 4 (belongs to X, signed in as Y): "Not syncing… nothing changes" — but every edit meanwhile mints Y-owned rows into X's library; if she then picks Sign out and later signs in as X, the library holds rows of two owners. (3) §3.6: a restored copy that predates a Move takes "the library's current … account" in its record while its rows keep the old owner — the record says Y, the rows say X.
  • Proposed fix. Ownership at mint follows the library's account (the record), not the session; the unowned-row backfill runs whenever the signed-in account equals the record's account; any landing whose rows' owner differs from the record's account runs the Move road (re-stamp + resyncReinitialize) inside its plan. Pin each of the three sequences.

M3 · §7 (migration), §9 (risks) · the declared road back is not one, the wait has no end, in-flight markers and other users' shapes are unaddressed.

  • (a) The road back strands. Step 3 removes the per-library-ID items (<scope>-<storeID>-bound / -biometric, Sources/VariorumCore/Persistence/StoreKey.swift:265-267). Rolling back = an older build + the A1 copy's files; the older build finds no per-ID item. A Standard library with an Apple wrap reopens through the Apple road (AppBootstrap.swift:350-357); a Castle library loses Touch ID (the lobby's biometryOffered needs gatedItemExists); an exposed library (no passphrase, no Apple copy — a Standard library whose Apple copy never landed) has no silent item and no wraps ⇒ openStore throws keyUnavailable ⇒ failedOutcome returns .failed(…, recovery: nil) (AppBootstrap.swift:448): doorless. And if the format-3 file is left beside the store, the older build's newest-mtime locate picks it and every unwrap throws newerFormat(3) (StoreKeyEnvelope.swift:407-411). Fix: leave the per-ID items in place (unread) for a release or more, or make "the road back" an in-app downgrade-safe act; never call the A1 copy alone the road back.
  • (b) "Wait" with no end. Step 1 waits for the vault when synced. Signed-out-with-sync-keys and deleted-account libraries are indeterminate indefinitely today (research §6b rows 20/23, S-19), so they never migrate — while §3.9 deletes the code that serves an unmigrated store (corner 45, RawKeyCustody, the gate's landing). Fix: define what assess returns with no record yet, or give the wait a terminal arm (e.g. signed out ⇒ record "account X, waiting for the vault", the custody shape as the interim mode).
  • (c) In-flight markers. A store carrying sync_state.rekey_in_progress at migration — including VX-235's unclearable one, which SyncEngine.refuseIfRekeyIncomplete honours forever — is not mentioned: resume first with today's runner, translate to a plan, or (VX-235) clear it on a proof that the store opens under the old key? Same for a live switchPending / thisDeviceOnlySwitch flag (step 5 retires them; the latter carries the honest Done).
  • (d) Shapes. The drills cover her Castle-and-synced stores ("the best-understood shape", §9) and the dev stores. 0.9.x users exist in every §6b shape (exposed, Standard-waiting, VX-202-race and VX-209 victims, no-biometry Castle, signed out with keys, deleted account), and "each migrates once, quietly". Fix: the migration's acceptance is a drill per old-build shape (the scenario test can generate them if it models the old state shapes, not only new acts). Note the Keychain leg (re-filing a real .biometryCurrentSet item) cannot be drilled from a test host on either real scope: KeychainTestTripwire refuses every test-process mutation of Variorum and Variorum-dev (VX-201 record, the F-U2-8 fixture refused). So "drilled first on byte-copies of your stores" covers the files only; name the throwaway-scope drill and the Debug-app run on the dev store (a held stage claim) that cover the Keychain.
  • (e) H before any secret is typed. H is minted at migration, but P and W are not typed at a Touch ID unlock, so a local-only Castle library's H sits only in the device item until the passphrase is next typed: backups made meanwhile carry an H no secret opens (rule 3's "on another Mac" fails for them), and a fingerprint change in that window invalidates the only copy. §9's "keeps every old key file until its key is safely in the history" must read "…in a history whose key carries a P or W wrap"; the first post-migration unlock should ask for the passphrase once (pre-13x §11 had that precedent: "the migration may ask ONCE").
  • (f) The ambiguous arm acts before it asks. Step 1 keeps today's reading (Standard) for corner 45's ambiguous case when no switch evidence survives — and an adoption leaves no evidence (it lands exactly one key file and moves the rest aside, LibraryAdoption.swift step 5) — VX-209's shape. (Today's route already lands the Apple wrap at the next launch when A is readable, so the libraries still ambiguous at migration are those where it was not.) The Standard repair then lands the wrap, and "the first launch says plainly which mode" only afterwards; §3.3's own rule treats an Apple wrap as unrecallable ("may have been copied elsewhere"). Fix: in the ambiguous case write nothing Apple-reachable until her answer (decision 35's "indeterminate writes nothing", applied here).

M4 · §3.3 ("Gate style is a fact, not a dial that can disagree: on a device with no Touch ID, Castle means Always ask"), §4 "A Mac without Touch ID", §11 ("Gate style follows the device") · "no Touch ID" is read from a check that is false transiently on Macs; enforced as a repair it destroys the Touch ID road of a lidded MacBook.

  • Evidence. Biometry is read as LAContext().canEvaluatePolicy(.deviceOwnerAuthenticationWithBiometrics, error: nil) (App/Variorum/Shared/StoreKeyGuard.swift:95-101). The settled design's own corner 36 records that this reports no biometry for "a closed MacBook on an external keyboard" and rules: "an existing biometric item on a lidded MacBook falls to the passphrase road for that open" — i.e. keep it. Today the transient reading already does damage in two places: the §11 migration persists Always ask and aligns custody to nowhere on !biometryAvailable (ProtectionMigration.swift:240-244), and the ceremony lands custody nowhere on !biometry (PostureSwitch.swift:1330-1333). Apple's own words (the lookup, CONFIRMED): canEvaluatePolicy — "Don't store the return value from this method because it might change as a result of changes in the system"; macOS 15's builtInSensorInaccessible — "Currently, the only example of this is a Clamshell Mode on macOS"; and the transient cases have their own codes (biometryDisconnected, biometryNotPaired, biometryLockout) beside biometryNotAvailable / biometryNotEnrolled.
  • Mechanism under the proposal. The reconciler runs at every input change, enforces the Castle repair "every key … lives behind Touch ID or nowhere" against the effective gate style, and "repairs only move toward the record". A reconcile while the lid is closed (or the Touch ID keyboard is unplugged, or biometry is locked out after failed attempts) reads "no Touch ID ⇒ Always ask" ⇒ the target is "nowhere" ⇒ the Touch ID item (holding the key and the history key) is removed, or her stored preference flips; every later open asks for the passphrase until she notices and re-enables Touch ID — the exact symptom §6 row 3 lists as a bug to fix.
  • Fix. Base "this Mac has no Touch ID" on a hardware/enrolment fact, never on a momentary canEvaluatePolicy false (distinguish the transient error codes); a repair never deletes a Touch ID item because biometry is unavailable now; the stored gate preference changes only by her press. Add lid-closed / keyboard-unplugged / lockout to the scenario test's action alphabet.

M5 · §8 (the phase), "Size, honestly" · the unit plan is outside the sizing guidance by a wide margin and says the opposite.

  • Evidence. notes/PhaseSizingGuidance.md: "Phase-sized = … a lockstep event, a posture model"; for custody/crypto "one unit is one road — a posture switch, a door, a recovery leg" with that road's drill as its evidence; "ONE acceptance unit per implementer session"; "at most TWO phase-sized units under ONE theme. A third is a split"; and "a plan … outside this guidance SAYS SO to her". U1 builds a new posture model (record, key file format 3, history, assess, reconciler, plan runner, catalog, route finder, the scenario world) yet is called "not phase-sized"; U2 re-roads every custody door on two platforms plus the migration (≈15 roads, each a unit by the guidance) in one unit; U3 bundles the restore list, the open rule, the offer's groups and re-encryption, the export ID, Data Safety/RESTORE.md, VX-211, VX-212 (a Sync/ mechanism) and VX-213. The postures phase this rebuilds took twelve implementer sessions (batches 1a–5b) and a two-round ladder.
  • Riskiest part / fallback. Unnamed. The riskiest is U2's rewire of the open path plus the quiet migration of strangers' stores (a defect there is a launch-time stranding). The fallback ("the minimal structural fix", §12) has no decision point; once U2 starts there is no clean place to fall back to.
  • Fix. Present it as a split (pre-13x's shape: one sitting, separate phase runs): the headless core as its own phase with the go/no-go for the fallback at its close; then road families as units with their own drills; the migration as its own unit with the per-shape drill (M3(d)); Sync/ items (VX-107/109/212) together under Fable.

M6 · §5 VX-212 ("the finishing device retires rows it proves no key can read, lists them in Diagnostics, and completes") · an automatic server-side deletion of user rows, specified in one line, against settled sync doctrine.

  • Evidence. The walk decrypts every row below the rotation seq and throws on failure (CorpusRotation .reencryptRemainder, Sources/VariorumCore/Sync/CorpusRotation.swift:592); the server's completion guard refuses while any ciphertext-carrying row sits below the seq (VARIORUM_ROTATION_ROWS_REMAIN, the header at :38-44). To complete, a row must move above the seq or disappear — pushing its unreadable bytes back moves it above the seq, where it fails EVERY device's pull (a pull decrypts the whole window with try window.map(crypto.decryptFromWire), Sources/VariorumCore/Sync/SyncEngine.swift:473, so one bad row refuses the round); the other road is tombstoning it with its content gone. A finishing device knows only C′ and C_prev, so "proves no key can read" is not provable (an older device may hold an older key). SyncSpec §6: "deleting an edit nobody chose to lose is the one forbidden outcome"; the proposal's §10 lists no SyncSpec change for it.
  • Fix. Design VX-212 separately in Sync/ (Fable) with its own canon line and her ruling: e.g. a named stuck state in which those rows stay sealed and are listed (never deleted), the previous key stays wrapped under M′ (no exposure — VX-191 §W.2), and decision 32's one-rotation-at-a-time refusal gains an explicit way out.

M7 · §3.3 Castle repair ("after a passphrase unlock on a device set to Touch ID, the Touch ID item is put back") and §6 row 3 · an automatic re-land after a biometry change removes the one protection .biometryCurrentSet gives against her stated attacker.

MINOR

m1 · §3.1 rule 4 ("Backups made while you were in Standard also open through your Apple account") · repeats the VX-263 overclaim the proposal diagnoses. Exposed-era Standard backups carry no Apple wrap (research §1.2 row "Exposed"; Posture.localReading's no-passphrase arm, StoreProtection.swift:104), and an A minted while iCloud Keychain is off stays on this Mac while it stays off (corner 46: "iCloud Keychain is off is undetectable on macOS"). And when two devices each mint A offline, iCloud Keychain keeps ONE and deletes the other (Apple's open-source CKKS conflict rule — lower UUID wins, no merge; LIKELY), so every wrap under the losing A stops opening through the Apple account; the proposal never states A's minting rule, and today's guards (mintAllowed, appleKeyWaiting, ProtectionController.swift:1636-1643) must survive into the reconciler. Fix: "Backups made while your library opened through your Apple account…", and let every surface take the sentence from the route finder.

m2 · §3.1 rule 3 and promise 5 · overclaim for copies made before the new build. Migration step 4 adds an old key to the history "the first time the passphrase is typed" — but the passphrase typed is today's, and an old key file is wrapped under the passphrase and words of its day (decision 11). A Castle-era pair whose key was retired before a passphrase change and a words regeneration never joins the history, so "every backup Variorum made of your library opens with [your passphrase], your twelve words, or Touch ID" is false for it (the route finder will honestly say "the passphrase you had on …", contradicting the rule). Also M3(e)'s window. Fix: scope rule 3 to backups made after the new build, and define promise 5's "a road the user still has" (which secrets the model assumes she keeps).

m3 · §3.1 rule 2 ("…an older library … comes back in the mode your library is in now") · false in two of the design's own arms. §3.6: "A different library keeps its own key and mode" — and an "older library" in Previous Libraries/ can be a different library (her ruled wording at #216: "older libraries Variorum kept when you opened or restored a different one"); and M1's newer-counter arm changes the mode. Fix: "A backup of your library…" and drop the counter arm (M1).

m4 · §3.6 confirm sentence ("Your library goes back to how it was on 12 October. It stays in Castle.") · overclaims for a synced library, where §3.6 itself says "after Resume Sync, changes made since on your other devices come back in". Fix: condition the sentence on the record's account (the 13x hold's own "catch up" wording).

m5 · §3.4 "A missing key file is not fatal" · the reconciler cannot rewrite what it says it rewrites. P and W wraps (and the history blob, which lives only in key files) need the secrets or a surviving key file; and with the key file gone the library ID — today read only from it (StoreIdentity.resolve, StoreIdentity.swift:33-41) — is unknown, so "what this device remembers … per library ID" needs a location binding the proposal never defines. A Castle library re-written with no P/W wrap is held only by the Touch ID item until the passphrase is typed. Fix: name the per-location pointer; the rewrite asks for the passphrase at once and re-seeds the history from the newest key file in the catalog.

m6 · §3.4 / §3.5 / §3.6 — plans. (a) "No backup is taken while a plan is in flight", but an enrolled Use Castle re-keys the library only after the server re-encryption completes (reencryptAndComplete then rekeyCustodyAndSnapshot, PostureSwitch.swift:764-811), so a rotation that stalls (offline after the vault write; VX-212) stops every scheduled backup with no sentence. Gate backups on the local-file window only. (b) "Everything a later step needs is captured up front" cannot hold for acts whose later steps need a secret (change passphrase, enroll, VX-100's "carry the word into the enrollment"): a plan on disk must never hold P, W or a derived key (pre-13x §3.1: never stored) — say which resumes ask again. (c) "No backup or copy can ever carry a half-finished act": Time Machine / Migration Assistant copy a plan file beside the library — mark it excluded from backup and let assess treat a plan whose world doesn't match as stale. (d) The open's order: landing the copy and then re-encrypting it puts an Apple-openable file at the store path for the window, which §4's synced VX-209 line says never happens; re-encrypt the staged copy straight into the store path instead. (e) Under "one key file per library", prepare-then-commit writes the NEW key file before the swap, so in that window the one key file names L′ while the store is still under L; §3.4's lobby derives roads from "this key" (the key file's) and would offer Touch ID / the passphrase for L′ — the VX-80 wrong-key class again — unless the route reads the plan first. Say that the plan names which key the store is under until its commit point, and that the open tries the history's keys.

m7 · §3.3 "Repairs only move toward the record" · decision 35 must be explicit. For a synced library whose vault is unread, "toward the record" with a stale record (another device entered Castle while this one was offline) would land the Standard Apple wrap — the window-phone incident decision 35 was written for. The assessment's "waiting for the vault" implies the guard; make it a rule: no repair that adds Apple reach runs on a synced library without a fresh vault read for its account.

m8 · §3.3 last bullet ("A synced library whose vault says another mode owes the follow — an act, never a silent repair") · ambiguous against pre-13x §7, where the follow into Standard is silent (nothing typed, a notice after). Say whether it stays automatic or now waits for a press.

m9 · §3.2 table + §7 step 3 · the device identity's move has no migration leg. §3.7 moves the sync identity out of the sync-keys item; §7 step 3 says that item "keeps its shape". Without a carry, every migrated device mints a new identity: a new device_cursor row while the stale one holds the purge horizon for up to 60 days (13j's drop-out), and a returner round (corner 37). Add the leg.

m10 · §3.2 ("a small local-only table inside the library file") and §7 ("the same road as the MCP grants and the search index") · the table's export/classifier consequences are unaddressed. Those two take different roads (MCP grants travel empty, StorePlaintextExport.swift:42-44,68-77; the search index is kept, SyncSchema.derivedTables); localOnlyTables is pinned as "exactly those four" (pre-13x §3.3; SyncSchema.swift:367-369) and the classifier calls a file an export when "ALL FOUR local-only tables" are absent (LibraryClassifier.swift:245-250). Separately, §3.6 says the plaintext export "will now carry the library's ID" without saying where (the record would be dropped with the local tables), and landing an export under the SAME ID on the same Mac reuses this Mac's sync identity with a cursor reset to 0 — a state 13j's purge design never covered (SPECULATIVE; mint a fresh sync identity on any landing that carries no sync_state).

m11 · §3.6 (the Castle offer: "Other libraries in Previous Libraries/ are not this library's business and are not listed") · narrows her ruling and leaves a gap unsaid. #216's ruled wording counts "older libraries Variorum kept when you opened or restored a different one" without restricting them to the same library; a different library's Standard-era copy stays Apple-openable with no sentence, and the VX-210 line ("Variorum only looks in its own folders") reads as if Variorum's own Previous Libraries/ were covered. m12 · §3.6 ("Bring them into Castle") · three survivors the offer doesn't name. iCloud Drive's Recently Deleted keeps a removed second-folder pair for 30 days (the delete offer already says so — PostureCopy.swift:569-571); the folder-level Backups/variorum-keys-<fp16>.json files (Apple-wrapped old keys, written once per key — StoreBackup.copyKeyEnvelope) are not pairs; and the 13p legacy escrow item — a RAW library key in iCloud Keychain — is left by today's migration when it is "another key's" (VX-191 §O.2), against design law 28 ("iCloud holds a KEY TO A WRAPPED COPY — never the copy"). The new migration is the natural place to remove escrow items for keys in the history.

m13 · §3.8 (the scenario test) · "we don't have to imagine them; the test generates them" overclaims. It explores the modeled action alphabet against fakes: real-Keychain classes (the data-protection vs file keychain split behind the 13p-fix loss, access-group identity, .biometryCurrentSet invalidation, iCloud propagation), adversarial inputs (M1(b)'s crafted copy) and B1's leaving-Castle reach are outside it. m14 · §3.7 ("If the account it belongs to no longer exists … the next sign-in says so") · not knowable by the client.
