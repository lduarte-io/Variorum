As built: is this the band?

Her words: “I don’t like that there’s a whole pale band around the quick click buttons rather than just hte buttons themsleves”. The quick-entry box has no button group (its only control is the “open ↵” door), so the capture walk looked for a band behind quick buttons and found one here.

The bar at rest (findings/W9-keyboard-bar-crop.png, from screens/60-editor-keyboard-bar-rest.png, shown at phone width). The page is cream (#FAF8F3); from the bar’s top edge to the keyboard a pure white strip (#FFFFFF) runs the full width, 52 pt tall, and the three glass buttons Aa · takes · block menu each sit in their own 62 × 48 glass shape on top of it. Two backgrounds for three buttons. The white is 1.06:1 against the cream, which is why it reads as “pale”.
The strip open (screens/61-editor-formatting-strip.png). Tapping Aa swaps the three buttons for eleven acts in one capsule, each 28 pt wide and 38 tall, on the same white band. She ruled this one-row shape (R22/R25) knowing it fell under 44 pt, to take the bar from 72 pt down to 52. Her newer words: “many of the button affordances are absolutely tiny”.

If she meant something else, the only other filled shape near quick buttons is the “✎ today” badge on the Journal’s shelf row; its fill is 1.05:1 against the room and close to invisible, so it is an unlikely reading (audit G §3.4).

Eleven 44 pt cells need 504 pt, which no iPhone has; that arithmetic is why R22 gave up the floor (MobileKeyboardBar.swift:128-141). Seven fit everywhere but Display Zoom, where they are still 15 pt wider than today’s.

My pick

LK-4: (a), drawn as one capsule. LK-3: (c).

  • Why the capsule. Her sentence asks for “just the buttons themselves”. Three scattered glass shapes over the last line of prose are busier than the band they replace; one capsule over the short end of the line is the least chrome that still looks pressable, and it makes the resting bar and the open strip one object that grows. It also gives the receipt line a home at the foot, out of the masthead. If she would rather not move Aa, plain (a) keeps every button where it is and still answers her words.
  • Why (c). It is the only option that keeps one row (her R22 reason) and reaches 44 pt (her newer complaint). The acts that keep a single tap are the ones that shape a paragraph (heading, lists, indent), which is most of what formatting on a phone is; the inline styles are also reachable by typing the marks, which the editor already shows.
  • What stays as it is. The glyphs’ meanings, the Aa door, the heading levels, the 4 pt of air above the keyboard that she set at R12, and the bar’s fixed 48 pt content row.
  • Not verified here. Whether prose can scroll behind a keyboard accessory, or whether floating needs the bar to become the app’s own view above the keyboard, is an implementation question for the ticket; these frames only show the look.
