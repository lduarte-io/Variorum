BK-1 — the Bookcase's phone shape, inside one wing. Today the phone has no shelves: 23 books from nine wings sit in one flat list, “Ideas inbox” twice, and “+ New Book” is the last row, hidden under the capture box. These frames show the Home wing's eleven books on its shelves (Writing, Reading & reference, Life, Welcome) plus the starred shelf, Unshelved and All. The 13i sitting ruled “the Bookcase/shelf strip as a list” but not the form.

Book rows are the same in every option: a 64 pt row, the name in Iowan 17, one meta line, and at most one mark at the trailing edge (the star, the finished ✓, or the count of arrivals waiting).

BK-1 (c) · pushedA shelf, pushed. The second level of (c). It is the same list as (a)'s solo, reached by a push instead of a chip; the shelf's own menu (rename, glyph, delete) lives in the round button.

My pick: (a), the chip row, solo by default with All as the last chip

It is the Mac's Bookcase read aloud on a phone — the same strip, the same solo-and-All rule, the same starred shelf leading — so the two devices teach each other. It is also the calmest answer to “everything just shows up in one bunch”: one wing, one shelf, four or five books, room to breathe. And because All is just the last chip, nobody who wants (b)'s one long list loses it.

Two things the strip needs to be honest on a phone: the row is 56 pt tall so a chip is a real target, and the strip scrolls back to show the solo'd chip whenever the screen appears. “New book” is a row at the end of the shelf it will land on and an entry in the round menu, never a row hidden under the capture box.
