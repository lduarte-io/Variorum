NT-5 — where commit and history live on a block, and the note’s masthead at sizes a thumb can hit

VM-44, grader 2. Two things on one page because they share a screen. The masthead comes first: it is where the worst touch targets in the app are, and what it keeps decides how much room the note has. Then the question itself: the doors to a block’s commits and history.

The masthead

As built the masthead holds eleven doors and every one is under 24 pt tall: the title (rename), four 14 × 14 icons 14 pt apart (copy · find · lock · note menu), the book line, and five word-chips (date · tags · more info · blocks · lens) on rows 4 pt apart. The title shares its row with the icons and is cut at about twenty characters. An empty system bar holding only the back button sits above all of it, and receipts appear as a seventh line that pushes the prose down and never leaves.

As built (screens/50-note-write-rest.png, at phone width). Hit areas, from the code: icons 14 × 14; chips the height of their text, 14 pt; title 23 pt (audit G, T38–T48).

All three proposals share one move: two tools stay as 44 pt buttons in the top bar (find, and the note menu), and the rest of the tools go inside that menu (copy note, seal, save a version, versions, blocks and the tray, lenses, references, delete). What stays visible are the note’s three facts. They differ in how the facts are shown and whether they stay on screen.

Today the pair is split. Commit take is in the block menu (the keyboard bar’s ⋯, which needs the caret in the block and the keyboard up). She placed the history door on the take card herself (batch 9). The face it opens is the one the Mac has since retired in favour of History and lineage.

The sheet they openA phone face for the Mac’s commits view, so the doors have somewhere to land: commit with an optional name at the top, a take filter, moments newest first, a moment opened in place with its three acts. This is one possible shape; the surface gets its own mockups (it is the largest piece of work on this page).

My pick

NT-5: (c), both doors. Masthead: (2), chips that scroll away under a compact bar.

  • Why (c). History belongs to the block, and most blocks have one take: the door has to be reachable without the takes browser, which rules out (b) alone. But a take card is where she is looking when she wonders what a take used to say, and she put the door there on purpose; keeping it costs one button. One sheet behind both doors means there is one history face to build, and it can be the Mac’s current one rather than the retired panel.
  • Commit beside history. Commit is the phone’s ⌘S. It should be the row nearest the thumb in the block menu and the first row of the History sheet, file once per press, and let her name the moment then or later.
  • Why masthead (2). It gives every door a 44 pt target and gives the title its full width, without making the note pay for it on every screenful. It also removes today’s hard cut where prose slides under the pinned masthead. (1) is the fallback if she wants the facts always in view; (3) hides them one step too far for someone who files and tags from the phone.
  • Open beside this. The block menu is reachable only with the keyboard up (W-136); where the markers sit (NT-10); the seal’s word (NT-4). None of them changes these picks.
