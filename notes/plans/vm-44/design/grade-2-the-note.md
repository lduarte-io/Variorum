# VM-44 — grade 2: everything inside a note

Draft working record, not canon. Grader 2's half: the Write surface at rest, the masthead, the note's sheets, the
keyboard bar and formatting strip, the block menu and structural receipts, takes, block history, comments and
callouts, the long note, and a book's Read page. Grader 1 has everything outside a note.

**The pass graded.** Study voice, light, Classic colours, iPhone 17 Pro simulator, default text size
(`atlas/screens/`, captions in `atlas/screen-inventory.md`). The phone cannot wear the sea-glass palette today;
that gap is recorded once (cross-cutting 12) and no screen is marked down for it. Sizes and hit areas are quoted
from `audit/G-visual-metrics.md` (cited as G, with its T-numbers) and not re-derived; walk findings are cited as W.

**Her direction.** "the overall look of the app is fine aside from how everything is weirdly small and spaced".
So the grades weigh size, spacing, touch and flow. A screen that already reads as the Mac's Study voice is not
marked down for style.

**What a grade means here.** A: right for a phone as it stands. B: sound, with small repairs. C: usable, but she
has to work at it. D: fails her on this axis. F: the axis is effectively absent.

## 3. Screen by screen: why, and the three most damaging problems

### 1. Write at rest, with the masthead — C−
`50-note-write-rest.png` · `66-editor-keyboard-down.png` · `23-quick-entry-opened-note.png` · `86-today-entry-keyboard.png`

- **L C+** the prose (Iowan 16, about 40 characters a line) reads well; everything above it is 11.5 pt at 2.1–3.4:1. **T F** eleven doors
  and none reaches 24 pt. **Cl C** bare words that do not look pressable; four unlabelled glyphs. **Ca B** quiet, but two header rows.
  **Cr C** white strips at the status bar and home indicator around a cream room (W4); a 16/28 line that is airier than it needs to be.
  **N C** no navigation title, no collapsing header. **F C+** writing is one tap; everything else is a hunt.

1. **The masthead is the app's worst touch surface, on the screen she lives in.** Four 14 × 14 pt icons in faint ink, 14 pt apart
   (copy · find · lock · ⋯), the book line and five word-chips each 14 pt tall, the two chip rows 4 pt apart (G T39–T48). Her words:
   "many of the button affordances are absolutely tiny".
2. **The title is cut so the icons can share its row.** "On keeping a commonplace book" shows as "On keeping a common…" on a 402 pt
   screen, and renaming it means tapping that 23 pt-tall truncated line.
3. **The chips are 11.5 pt in faint and muted ink and do not read as controls.** "no date", "more info", "blocks" and "lens" are words
   with no shape; "no date", "more info" and "lens" are faint at 2.1:1. Her words: "weirdly tiny font a lot of the time". On the
   journal entry (`86-…`) the title and the date chip both read the raw "2026-11-12".

### 2. Keyboard bar at rest — C
`60-editor-keyboard-bar-rest.png` · `findings/W9-keyboard-bar-crop.png`

- **L C** Aa is readable; the takes glyph is a hairline about seven points high inside a 62 pt cell. **T A−** 62 × 48 pt glass cells: one of
  only four control sites in the app that pass 44 pt (G T55). **Cl C** nothing says the middle glyph means takes; this ⋯ and the masthead's ⋯ open
  different menus. **Ca D** a full-width white strip between the page and the keyboard. **Cr C−** two backgrounds for three buttons; the
  strip's white is not the room's cream. **N B−** an input accessory with glass is native; a band plus glass is not. **F B** block acts are
  two taps once the keyboard is up.

1. **This is her band.** "I don't like that there's a whole pale band around the quick click buttons rather than just hte buttons
   themsleves": a 52 pt-tall #FFFFFF strip runs the full width behind three glass shapes on a #FAF8F3 page (W9; G §3.4).
2. **The middle button's glyph is nearly invisible.** The takes mark is a faint 14 pt glyph in a 62 pt cell; the largest target on the
   screen carries its smallest sign.
3. **Two ⋯ on one screen mean two things.** The masthead's opens note actions; the bar's opens the block menu.

### 3. Formatting strip — D+

2. **Low-contrast, uneven glyphs.** Muted ink at 14 pt, with one glyph visibly larger than the rest.

## 4. Cross-cutting problems (the design-language sheet's agenda)

1. **One fixed caption size carries everything that is not prose.** 241 of 352 type sites are 11.5 pt (G §0), and inside a note that
   includes things she reads as content: her blocks in the block list, comment text (13), explainers, dates. It is the Mac's sidebar size
   on a touch screen. Her words: "weirdly tiny font a lot of the time".
2. **Two inks below reading contrast.** Muted is 3.4:1 and faint 2.1:1 on the Study room (G §4.2), and they set the chips, icons, hints,
   explainers and inactive words. Small and pale compound each other.
3. **Words as buttons.** Almost every act in the note's sheets is a plain 11.5 pt word whose hit area is its own glyphs, 14 pt tall: Done
   (on every sheet), Put back, remove, diverge, Edit, Delete, Select, Unlock Note. Her words: "many of the button affordances are
   absolutely tiny". Acts that undo each other sit side by side at that size (Edit/Delete, diverge/remove).
4. **The masthead stack.** An empty system bar with a back button, then a title that shares its row with four icons, a book line, two rows
   of chips, a sealed line when sealed and a receipt line when something happened. It is pinned, it grows, and prose is cut under it.
5. **Three sheet grammars on one screen.** Six SwiftUI sheets with hand-rolled "Title … Done" headers, three hand-built overlays (takes,
   comments, history) with no grabber and no swipe down and a different, smaller header, and system alerts used as forms. Sheets holding
   two lines open full height; only note actions has a detent.
6. **Doors hidden behind state or gesture.** The block menu needs the keyboard up; heading levels need a long-press; history is under a
   take card's ⋯; the tray is under "blocks"; nothing has a swipe action.
7. **Receipts in the wrong place.** They appear in the masthead, far from the act, push the page down, never leave, and the one that most
   needs an Undo has none.
8. **Three marker styles for one idea.** Takes (faint glyph, right gutter, narrows the column), comments (brown dot and number at the last
   line's end, 10.5 pt), callouts (a glyph inside the text). None is large enough to press and they do not look related.
9. **Unpainted edges.** White behind the status bar, under the home indicator and behind the keyboard bar and find bar, around a cream
   room (W4, W9). The keyboard bar's band is the one she named; it is the same defect three times.
10. **Vocabulary that asks her to already know.** diverge, lens, more info, blocks (for the tray), "no date", raw ISO days as titles, and
    two different ⋯.
11. **Nothing in the chrome answers the text-size setting** (W19); in a note only the prose and take text scale.
12. **Recorded once, not graded per screen.** The phone shows Classic colours and cannot wear the sea-glass palette; every list that
    crosses wings shows twins. Both are absent features (audits A and G), not failures of these screens.

## 5. What is good and must survive

- **The prose.** Iowan Old Style on a quiet page with a sensible measure, boundaries invisible at rest, markdown marks shown in a quieter
  ink. In the Study voice the writing surface itself needs a point of size and less line air, not a redesign.
- **Calm by default.** A note at rest shows words and almost nothing else. The fix for the tiny chrome is fewer, larger doors, not more
  chrome.
- **Swipe to peek, tick to choose** (`71-…`). One take at a time, in place, is the phone's own grammar for takes and it works. Keep the
  gesture and the pill's wording ("take 2/3 · warmer"); enlarge and reposition the pill.
- **The takes browser's cards** (`72-…`): real prose in each card at the body size, a clear Chosen / Peeking state, labels. Keep the
  content; replace the container and the control sizes.
- **Callouts** (`82-…`): a soft wash behind the block reads at once and stays calm.
- **The book's Read page** (`42-…`): the best screen in this half. Keep the cream page, the serif, the title hierarchy and tap-to-write.
- **System menus and alerts where they are used**: legible, pressable, and consistent. The heading and block menus only need better doors
  and grouping.
- **The system find bar**: adopting the platform's control gave the one fully sound tool in the note.
- **The words.** The copy is careful and honest: "The note goes to the Trash for 30 days. You can put it back from there until then.",
  "Leaving — on the 30-day clock", the Versions explainer. It deserves to be readable.
- **The keyboard bar's three resting buttons** are the right three, at a size that already passes.
- **Speed where it was measured**: the 40,000-word note opens with its text in place.
