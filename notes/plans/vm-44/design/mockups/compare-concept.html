Compare on the phone concept, after v1

VM-44, a concept only. Her words: “having thought about it I would like to have a compare mode, maybe with the notes as different tabs that you can swipe between and still have the notes line up block by block? that’s just one idea on it and again, probably won’t even be in v1 of the mobile app”. This reverses the earlier exclusion (MobileSoul §4.5), so it is an open space led by her idea. Nothing here is in v1. Frames read left to right.

What Compare is on the Mac: up to four notes side by side, their blocks paired by lineage (a duplicate’s blocks remember the blocks they came from), never by order or by matching text; gaps only where that lineage speaks; a note’s duplicates and saved versions offered first. Seeded content: Essays ▸ “On keeping a commonplace book” and its duplicate “On keeping a commonplace book — talk”, drawn here cut down for a talk: one block reworded, one shortened, one cut, one added.

A — her idea: the notes as tabs, lined up block by block

Two notes, one tab each. Swipe sideways to change tabs; the page does not move up or down, so the same block sits at the same height in both. Here: the essay, and the copy she cut down for a talk.

What is easy

  • The pages. Compare only reads, so no page holds a live editor (P3 holds without effort). The phone already draws a note’s blocks this way.
  • The washes. TextCompare is in Core and the History pane already draws them.
  • The doors. The words are shared; each door is one row.
  • Takes against chosen (T). Every brain is in Core and the peek exists. Size S.

What is hard on a phone

  • Row heights. A row is as tall as its tallest version, which is only known once both are laid out. Rows off screen are estimates, so a long note can shift under the thumb. The build must measure only the rows on screen (P1) and hold the top visible row still.
  • Tall empty places. A long block missing from one note leaves a third of the screen blank (CMP-1).
  • The swipe. It competes with the system’s back swipe at the left edge, and with the take peek, which is also a sideways swipe. Compare pages do not peek.
  • Copying a block into another note. No operation does it; only Snippets copy into a note today. Adding as a take on a matching block exists (CMP-2).
  • A divide. One block here can be two there; the row stacks the two. A moved block is marked, never guessed.
  • More than two notes. What each tab’s marks are measured against needs a rule (A9).
  • Tab names. A note and its duplicate have nearly the same title, so their tabs shorten to nearly the same words (A9). A saved version’s date is clearer than a duplicate’s name.
  • Rewrites. Word washes say little when a take or a block was rewritten whole.

What I would build first

  • First: T, takes against chosen (W-177, S). It needs no hoist and proves the washes on the phone.
  • Then: A, her tabs, for two notes, read-only, with padded rows (W-186, L). It maps one-to-one onto PostAlignment’s rows, each page stays a real note, and it grows to three or four notes.
  • (c) the hold comes almost free on top of A (the same pages, a hold instead of a swipe). Add it later if she wants it.
  • (b) the single column is the cheapest to build, with no heights to match. I would keep it as the fallback if A’s row matching feels rough on a device, not as the first choice: it doubles the page and only works for two.

Open questions for the author

  • SC-1. Which shape: (a) tabs, (b) one column, (c) the hold? And is T, the takes piece, built first? My pick: T, then (a).
  • CMP-1. A missing block: a box as tall as the block, so everything lines up (A3), or one line and only the top block lines up (A3 alt)? My pick: A3.
  • CMP-2. Copying across. Is “add as a take on the matching block” the right meaning of copying a block over? And may a block be copied into a note where it is missing (a new operation)?
  • CMP-3. Is Compare read-only on the phone, with Open this note as the way to edit? The Mac edits in place. My pick: read-only.
  • CMP-4. Two notes only at first, or up to four as on the Mac? My pick: two.
  • CMP-5. Should each page wash only its own words (drawn), or also show the other note’s words struck through? My pick: its own only.
  • CMP-6. The Mac’s Compare also holds Outline (make an outline note beside the note). Not drawn here; does it come to the phone at all?
