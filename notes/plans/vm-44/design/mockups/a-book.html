VM-44 mockups — a book

VM-44 mockups — inside one book. Revised after the first sitting: every frame follows plans/sitting-1-rulings.md. Today the phone’s book is a list of bare titles with two text links at its foot: no previews, no pin mark, no sign of what is waiting, none of the finished / sealed / owned / archived family, one “Book Details” half-sheet with two acts, and a Finish that silently dismisses arrivals she never saw.

“Chip row above the list” · shelf management: “Full, behind a ⋯” · book views in v1: “Contents and Read” · the book strip: “At the foot” · “Simple editor” · word target and note limit: “Show and edit both” · the Journal’s new entry: “Foot of the Journal” · “Open Arrivals first”: “Keep it”

What was chosen, in one line each. Shelves are a chip row; managing them is in full, behind a ⋯. A book has two views in v1: Contents and Read. A note opened from a book carries the book strip at its foot. The rule editor is the simple one. Book settings show and edit the word target and the note limit. The Journal’s “+ New entry” is at its foot. Finish says how many arrivals it dismisses and offers “Open Arrivals first”. A muted pin marks pinned rows; “Move to chapter” is in the note’s menu. The bottom bar shows on every list here and never inside a note. Lines: none for ordinary acts. Words in the frames are the Mac’s own, in sentence case; the drafts are in design/wording-list-1.md. Arrivals has its own file, mockups/arrivals.html.

The tyranny of the blank page
in Blog drafts · #blog #draft

The blank page is not empty. It is full of everything you have not decided yet.

That is why it is heavy. A page with one bad sentence on it weighs almost nothing: the sentence can be fixed, or crossed out, or kept as a warning. A page with no sentence on it is still carrying every sentence it might have had.

So I have stopped starting with the page. I start with a list of what the piece will not be about, and the page gets lighter with each line.

The trick, if it is one, is to decide something small and wrong on purpose. The second decision is always easier than the first.

Why I still print things
in Blog drafts · #blog

Paper shows me the shape of a piece before I have read a word of it.

On a screen every paragraph is the same height: the height of the screen. On paper the long one looks long, and I can see from across the room that the middle is where the trouble is.

I print, I walk away, I come back and read it standing up. Standing up is the important part.
