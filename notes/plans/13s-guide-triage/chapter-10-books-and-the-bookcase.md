## Lesson: the classics walk

### Final text

Three kinds of thing live on your Bookcase, and this whole chapter rests on telling
them apart. A gathering shows you every note matching its rule; it holds nothing. A
book holds its notes as members. An owned book is the only place its notes live.

Two of the three are waiting on the Welcome shelf: this guide, a plain book, and the
adventures of sherlock holmes, an owned book. The third kind is with your unshelved
books: the starred notes gathering. A gathering is made from a rule and holds nothing,
and this one's rule is the star. Star any lesson (right-click it ▸ Star) and open the
gathering: the note shows there without moving anywhere. Unstar it and it leaves on its
own.

Look closer at Sherlock: every note in it wears a quiet "owned by" mark naming its
book. That book owns its notes. They live there and nowhere else. This guide is the
plain kind: a book whose notes are ordinary members, free to live in other books too.
Sherlock's notes also all wear the tag #classics, and Help ▸ Restore has two more
classics doors: one adds "\(SampleLibraryJekyllBook.name)", a second owned classic
whose notes arrive wearing the same tag, and one adds a classics gathering made from
that tag, which shows every note wearing it without holding a single one of them.

Click a Sherlock note's mark and you land in its book, at that very note. A gathering
is a window onto notes that live somewhere; the mark is the door to where they live.

## Lesson: a book from the inside

### Final text

You are inside a book right now: an ordered collection of notes with real chapters. The
chapter list on the left is workable: ＋ new chapter, right-click a chapter to rename,
move, or delete it, and drag the ⣿ dots (in the list or on the page's chapter headings)
to reorder chapters. ＋ new note mints a note right inside the book. Notes show as
collapsed cards: clicking a card opens the note in the full editor; its ▸ expands it in
place, right here.

Four views of a book share the toggle at the top. Outline is this scroll, and it is
where a book gets written: chapter headings, notes you can collapse, and the same
editor you write a note in. Index keeps a column of the chapters and notes beside the
whole text; click any block in the text and it goes live right where it is. Book is a
two-page spread. Each page is one whole note, and the page you are in is editable as
Write or as its takes; ◀ ▶ turn the leaf two notes at a time, and ◁ ▷ nudge by one so
you can re-pair. Read is for reading only, in two faces: scroll, the whole book as one
calm continuous page, and pages, real book pages with the text flowing across them.
Turn the leaf, or scrub. A finished book opens into Read by itself.

A book can carry a word target (right-click it on the Bookcase ▸ Length & Extent…).
This one wears one, so its row on the Bookcase shows progress. A single note can wear
its own target too: click the word counts in the editor's bottom bar. Targets are a
measure, never a wall.

## Lesson: membership, not ownership

### Final text

Editing a note that is in a book is always editing every book it appears in, owned or
not. Adding a note to a book never copies it. A copy only ever happens because you
asked for one, and it always remembers where it came from.

Try it, start to finish. On the Bookcase press ＋ New Book and make a book called
"practice"; inside it, ＋ new chapter, then ＋ new note, and write a line. Make a
second book the same way. Now put that same note in the second book too: click the line
naming the note's book at the top of its page, and the panel it opens has Add to Book.

Edit the note from either book and look at the other: it changed too, because it is one
note in two books. Then delete one of the books: right-click it on the Bookcase ▸
Delete Book…. Deleting a plain book never touches a note. Yours is still there, in the
other book and in the Library.

A deleted book waits \(TrashWindow.days) days in the Trash (the trash icon at the top
of the Library) with a Put back door. Its chapters and memberships come back with it.
The only way a note dies is deleting the note.

## Lesson: gatherings & arrivals

### Final text

A gathering is made from a tag. Remove the tag and the note leaves on its own. You
can't rearrange a gathering: bind it into a book to take it in hand, and it keeps
collecting after you do.

Try it with two of this chapter's own lessons. Give this lesson and the next one a tag
of your own, say #trial, with the ＃ on the masthead. On the Bookcase, ＋ New Book ▸
Create Gathering…, and gather from #trial: both lessons show. Take the tag off one and
it leaves the gathering at once.

Now right-click the gathering on the Bookcase ▸ Bind into a Book…. It becomes a book
holding the lesson it showed, and it keeps collecting: tag the other lesson #trial
again and the book takes it in. Being a book, it can wear your own order: drag one note
above the other. Then take the tag off either one. It stays. A book's notes are
members, and untagging never sends a member away.

A collecting book auto-adds freely; anything it can't seat on its own waits at
Arrivals, the little tray beside the book's settings gear, for you to judge: add it,
copy it in, or dismiss it. Finishing a book dismisses whatever is still waiting.
Changed your mind? Add it by hand; dismissing only stops the book from adding it on its
own.

## Lesson: owned, finished, sealed

### Final text

Put a note in an owned book and that book is its home. Want it elsewhere too? You'll
get a copy, and you'll know. Remove it and it goes back to the Pool, whole.

Try it on your "practice" book: right-click it on the Bookcase ▸ Own Its Notes…. Now
add its note to another book: the app stops and offers the copy, and says so. Then,
inside the book, right-click the note ▸ Release from This Book: it goes to the Pool,
whole. (On a book that doesn't own its notes the same door reads Remove from This
Book.)

Finished means the table of contents is done; the writing inside can still change.
Sealed means the book is done. Both doors are on the book's right-click and in its Book
Settings…: Finish and Seal…, and Reopen and Unseal take them back.

Deleting a plain book never touches a note. Deleting an owned book asks: keep the notes
(they go to the Pool) or take them too. The Trash gives back whatever went in together,
together.

## Lesson: the bookcase

### Final text

Your books live on shelves, alphabetical until you choose otherwise. The shelves run
left to right exactly as All stacks them top to bottom. Unshelved books wait at the top
until you file them.

The shelves are the strip across the top of the Bookcase: All, then starred, then each
shelf by name. Welcome is the one this guide came on. Starred isn't a shelf you file
into: it shows every starred book, wherever it's shelved, and it can't be renamed or
deleted. ＋ shelf makes another; right-click a shelf to rename it, give it a glyph,
make it the default, or delete it (its books just return to Unshelved). The order ▾
beside them reads A–Z or last edited.

Try it: drag a book onto a shelf's name to file it there (or right-click the book ▸
Move to Shelf). Switch the order ▾ to last edited and the books you worked in most
recently come first. Then star a book (right-click ▸ Star): starred books float first,
under any order, and starred gathers them all in one place.

The Bookcase is a place, arranged by you, and it stays where you put it. Finding is the
Library's work: search and the day filter live there, never here.

## Lesson: the journal & the pool

### Final text

The journal is an ordinary book that is special in one way: it is where ⌘J and Today's
Entry file. It is made the first time you ask for a day's entry, and from then on you
can archive it but you can't delete it, and it comes back when a day arrives. Today's
Entry sits at the top of the Bookcase; the journal itself waits among your unshelved
books until you file it.

The Pool is every note with no book. Notes that show in gatherings tuck behind the
count in its masthead; one click shows them.

See it happen: press ⌘⇧N, type a line, and close the pad. What you wrote is kept as a
note, tagged "quicknote", and the quicknotes gathering appears on your Bookcase to show
it. The note itself is in no book, so it lives in the Pool, tucked behind the count
because a gathering shows it. Open the Pool, click show, and there it is.

## Lesson: wings

### Final text

A wing is a separate library in the same app: a different room in the same house.
Nothing crosses between wings except what you move or copy yourself.

Most separation wants a shelf. A wing is for when you want a whole separate library:
the Bookcase's masthead names the one you're in (Home, to begin with) and its menu has
New Wing…. Switch, and everything shows that wing alone: the Bookcase, the Library, the
Pool, your tags, search.

Once a second wing exists, a note's right-click carries Move to Wing and Copy to Wing,
and a book's carries Move to Wing. Those are the only crossings. Home holds the
journal, your quick notes and this guide, and it can't be deleted.
