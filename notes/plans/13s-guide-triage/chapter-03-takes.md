## Lesson: your first take

### Final text

The fan is the reason this app exists: a block can hold several parallel takes, whole
alternative texts, side by side, none of them lost, for as long as you like. Most
blocks live with one take; the ones you're unsure about can hold ten.

The next paragraph can't decide what it wants to be. It's yours to fan out.

The lighthouse keeper counted the ships going out, and never once the ships coming
home.

Press ⌘2 for Takes, click into that paragraph and press ⌘T (or ＋ take in the bottom
bar): a second take appears beside it, born as a copy of what you were reading.
Rewrite it freely: bolder, quieter, backwards. The first take is untouched. ⌥⌘T makes a
blank take instead, for a genuinely different attempt.

Notice: exactly one pane is ever live for typing, the one with the ring. Click any take
to make it the live one. Your keystrokes only ever land in the ring.

## Lesson: choosing & the board

### Final text

Takes are for trying; choosing is deciding. The take with the ✓ is what the note
actually says. Visible is what you're looking at; chosen is what the note is. ⌘⏎
chooses the take you're in; nothing else changes the note.

The block below carries four takes of one sentence, ready for practice. In Takes (⌘2),
open its fan (⌘⇧T), step through them with ⌃⌘← and ⌃⌘→, and choose your favorite.

The rain came in from the sea without an opinion about any of us.

When a fan holds more takes than fit, the strip scrolls freely sideways and pages:
◀ 1–2 of 4 ▶. ⌥⌘← and ⌥⌘→ turn the pages from the keyboard, and the counter always
agrees with what you scrolled to. Take cards keep one steady height; a long take
scrolls inside its card. The style: menu in the board's top line picks how fans open
(stack, carousel, margin grid, or slot split), and ⎋ closes every fan at once.

## Lesson: keeping takes safe

### Final text

Everything autosaves, always. A commit is the deliberate one: ⌘S keeps a savepoint of
the take you're in, a version you can always come back to, no matter what you type over
it. Commits only ever add; nothing overwrites one.

Try it here: click into this paragraph, change a few words, press ⌘S. A small field
appears: type a name for the moment, "before the rewrite", or just press ⏎ to save it
unnamed. Then press history in the bottom bar, right beside commit. Your commit is
listed under this take, its name first where it has one. Restore loads any commit back
into the take. Name (or Rename) changes what it is called; the name is always editable,
the saved text never is. And committing again just adds another.

The block below has a spare take to practice archiving on. Click into the second take
and press ⌘⇧A. It leaves the board, out of the fan but never gone.

Never gone means never gone: an "archived · 1" door appears right under that block's
takes. Open it and Unarchive puts the take straight back in the fan, no leaving the
board. (Write's takes panel keeps the same archived list, if you're over there.)

You are not the only one committing. Leaving a note (closing it, switching away,
quitting) quietly commits each take you changed while you were in it, and a note you
leave open gets a commit after about two hours. A take you haven't changed is never
saved twice. The quiet ones are muted and fold into a single "N quiet commits · show"
line, so the ones you took yourself stay the ones you see. Settings (⌘,) ▸ Writing has
the one switch: every time you leave, only after a note sits untouched, or only when
you press ⌘S. Whichever you pick, the app still saves a take right before anything
could put your words out of reach.
