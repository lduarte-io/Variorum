## Lesson: divide

### Final text

Divide cuts a block in two at your cursor: the everyday gesture for "this part should
stand on its own so I can work on it separately." The cut can land between any two
words. It snaps to the nearest word edge, settles a little more readily onto sentence
ends and line breaks, and the app shows you where it landed. Only a single word has
nothing to divide.

This block holds two thoughts and they deserve two blocks. Put your cursor right before
this second sentence and press ⌘⇧D.

If the block has several takes, ⌘⇧D still just cuts: the take you're looking at splits
across the two new blocks, and every other take stays whole on the first block, the one
you divided. Nothing goes missing and there's nothing to hunt for. When you'd rather
send them somewhere else, or place a cut in each take by hand, that's Divide choosing
takes, ⌥⌘⇧D, and it has the next lesson to itself.

From Takes, divide acts right there: press ⌘⇧D, or Divide in the bottom bar's block ▾
group, and the block you're in is cut without leaving the board. (Extract, Make Block
and Merge still travel to Write and act there, visibly. That carry is a feature, not a
trick.)

One ⌥⌘Z (structural undo) puts a divided block back together. The two undos lesson at
this chapter's end has the whole story.

## Lesson: divide choosing takes

### Final text

Dividing a fanned block raises a real question: each take is a different text, so where
does each one go? ⌘⇧D answers it the quick way: cut the take you're looking at, leave
the rest whole on the first block. When the answer matters, ask for the choice instead.
⌥⌘⇧D lays every take out in one sheet and asks where they should go.

The block below carries three takes, staged for it. Put your cursor mid-block and press
⌥⌘⇧D. The sheet titled "Divide choosing takes" opens on a row of four: keep the takes
on the top block (the everyday answer, already chosen), keep them on the bottom, copy
them to both, or "Choose per take…", the one you really have to ask for. Pick that
fourth one: every take gets a checkbox and its text laid out with a ✂ you can place
anywhere between words. Drag it, click a spot, or use ←/→ (⌥ jumps by sentence, ⇥
moves between takes). Leave the third take unchecked before you confirm.

The ferry crossed at dawn. Nobody on board spoke about the storm.

Confirm makes two blocks: the first holds every checked take's first half, the second
the endings, so your openings and endings stay mix-and-matchable. A take doesn't have
to split at all: park its ✂ at the very end and the whole take rides into the first
block (at the very start, the second). The unchecked take didn't vanish: it stays with
the original block, preserved whole off-stage, and ⌥⌘Z puts the whole thing back as it
was.

## Lesson: extract & make block

### Final text

Extract pulls a span at a block's edge out into its own block, no fuss at all: the
source block keeps its identity and all its takes. Select from anywhere in this
paragraph to its end and press ⌘⇧E (the selection's edges snap to the nearest word
edge, settling gently onto sentence ends). This closing line is begging to stand alone.

A middle span can't extract (the two ends would be left holding hands), so Make Block
(⌘⇧K) is the tool there: it runs two divides as one undoable act. The lonely sentence
in the shaping blocks lesson was exactly this gesture.

Both work from Takes: make your selection in the take you're reading, press the key,
and the app carries the selection to Write and acts. If the block is fanned, the
selection carries approximately. The app snaps to boundaries and never cuts silently
where it isn't sure.

## Lesson: merge

### Final text

Merge is divide's opposite: neighboring blocks become one. The seam between them
becomes a line break inside the merged block, so the page reads exactly as it did.
Divide at a paragraph break and merge it back, and you get precisely what you started
with.

These two short blocks are really one thought.

They have been waiting for your ⌘⇧M since the day they were seeded.

In Write, put the cursor at the boundary between them (⌘⇧B shows seams) and press ⌘⇧M,
or select across both and merge the whole run as one undoable act. From Takes, Merge
joins the block you're in upward, into the one above.

When either block has other takes, Merge brings them all along: the two takes you were
looking at join, and every other take rides onto the merged block whole. Nothing is
left behind to hunt for, and the extra copies are quick to delete. To choose instead,
use Merge choosing takes (⌥⌘⇧M). It asks first: bring every take (the everyday answer,
already chosen), only the two you were looking at, or pair by hand, which opens
pre-paired with the two takes you were looking at, so ⏎ still gives you exactly the
everyday join. Pair takes with ＋ and the merged block gets one joined take per pairing
(divide a two-take block, pair 1+1 and 2+2, and it comes back whole); check "carry" to
ride a take over unjoined; each pairing previews its join: space, new line, or nothing.
Takes are never multiplied combinatorially. Pairing is choosing by hand.

When you pair by hand, whatever you leave unpaired stays with the released blocks in
lineage. The app names the road, and ⌥⌘Z brings the sources back, takes and all, from
either road. Merging three or more blocks at once asks the same question with two
answers only (every take, or only the shown ones) and never opens the pairing surface.
The block below carries a spare take so you can watch both: ⌘⇧M brings the spare along,
⌥⌘⇧M asks where the takes should go.

This block has a second take. Merge me upward and my spare comes along whole; with
⌥⌘⇧M you are asked first, my takes laid out for pairing if you want them.

## Lesson: the two undos

### Final text

Variorum keeps two undo stacks, deliberately separate. ⌘Z is text undo: your typing,
inside the take you're editing, as in any editor.

⌥⌘Z is structural undo: divides, merges, extracts, moves, deletions of blocks and
takes. It never touches your typing; it restores the shape. Every structural act in
this book is one ⌥⌘Z away from undone, and the report of what was undone lands at the
bottom of the window. ⇧⌥⌘Z is its redo: undo an undo, as many times as you like.

The two stacks never mix, and the app helps you at the junction: press ⌘Z when there is
nothing left to undo in the text and a structural change is what you actually did last,
and the status line says so and names ⌥⌘Z. The key still does what ⌘Z always does; the
app only puts up a sign.

If a structural undo would overwrite words you typed after the act, the app asks first.
That is the one undo that sets writing aside, so it is the one that stops to check. Say
yes and a commit of your newer text is kept anyway: insurance, so nothing typed is ever
more than one restore away. Every other structural undo stays silent. Try it: divide
the block above, type a few words into the first half, press ⌥⌘Z, answer the question,
then look in history.
