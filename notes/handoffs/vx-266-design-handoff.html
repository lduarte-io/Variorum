1. A follower's anchor is also locked under the secret typed at its follow (design §3.4, rule 5 in §3.1)

The problem it answers (the late extra read's MAJOR 3). Your ruling, verbatim: "i think that it's probably good to leave it fixed on the day you left ... c opies before you left castle open with the passphrase you had when you lefti s solid." On the Mac that presses Use Standard, that's exactly true. On your other devices it often wasn't: each device's anchor was locked only with what that device happened to hold, so it carried locks that could be stale. For example:

  • you change the passphrase on the Pro;
  • the Air keeps its key under the old one, opening by Touch ID every day;
  • you leave Castle on the Pro;
  • the Air's copies from before the leave would then open with the old passphrase, not the one you had when you left.

The decision: when a device follows you out of Castle, it types one secret anyway, for the new master. That same secret now also locks its anchor as the anchor is written. So each device's older copies open at least with the secret you typed there to follow. Rule 5 now says so:

"Your other devices each follow with one secret typed, so their own copies from before open with the words, and with the passphrase when it was the one you typed there to follow; each copy's Opens with line says which, and from which day."

Why:

  • It's free: the secret was just typed.
  • It only adds a road, under a secret nothing Apple-reachable holds.
  • It makes your sentence true at each device's own leave instead of false on every follower.
  • It isn't the dead "anchor follows your secrets" rule: nothing re-locks an anchor after it's written. This lock is part of writing it.

The other option: change nothing, and only say the truth — on a device that followed, copies from before open with whatever opened that device that day, often not the passphrase you had when you left. The Opens with line would name it.

The residue either way (design §8): words made new during Castle while a device was offline, or a passphrase changed elsewhere and never typed there, can leave a follower's older copies opening only with that day's other secret. Their Opens with line names what opens them.

2. "Off in Release" means the app's wiring (design §5)

The decision: your words were "wait to turn the new system on in release builds / wait to put it in release builds til i'm ready to release it." I read "off in Release" as: the new core is compiled into Release builds but dormant, and nothing uses it until your switch flips the wiring. It's tested in both build configurations from phase 1.

Why: that way no new code meets Release's optimizer for the first time at the switch, the riskiest moment, and the code needn't sit behind compile flags for months. Nothing new runs in Release either way.

The other option, the literal one: leave the new code out of Release builds entirely until the switch, with Release -configuration test runs from phase 1 as the mitigation. If you'd rather, it's a one-line change to §5 and the phase-1 plan's §2.

3. How the migration's real-scope branch is rehearsed (design §5 and §9, open)

Debug builds use the dev store's keychain scope, and tests are barred from your real one, so the migration's fold through your real old shared Apple key would otherwise run for the first time on your own store. The design requires rehearsing that branch before your live stores migrate. The method is yours, decided at VX-329's plan. The options:

Whatever runs logs every Apple key id it mints, so a stray key can be deleted by id.

5. The Touch ID re-arm question asks only after a press fails on an item that exists (design §3.10)

The problem (the extra read's MINOR 6). Someone with your login password copies the library and memory item while you're on Touch ID. You switch to Always ask. They enrol their finger and write the copies back. Both copies say "Touch ID", no item exists, and round 3's question — "Touch ID can't open Variorum on this Mac any more. Set it up again?" — would appear by itself. A yes makes an item their finger opens.

The decision: the question follows only a press that fails on an item that exists, which is the case of a fingerprint change invalidating it. When the choice says Touch ID but no item exists — deleted, a wiped keychain, or that rollback — nothing is asked. Always ask is recorded quietly, the lobby's quiet line says Touch ID isn't set up for this library, and you turn Touch ID back on in Settings with the passphrase.

Why: a question that appears by itself can be steered; a press you make in Settings can't.

The cost: after a genuine keychain wipe, you turn Touch ID back on in Settings instead of answering a prompt. Your VX-310 rule, the button whenever an item exists, is untouched.

The other option: keep round 3's question for every missing item, and add to the stated limit "unless you answer yes to Touch ID's question".

6. A new device identity starts on Always ask (design §3.10)

The decision: a replacement Mac (Migration Assistant, Quick Start) has no act and no press of yours to make its Touch ID item, so it starts on Always ask. You turn Touch ID on in Settings.

Why: it's consistent with call 5: no item-making question appears by itself.

The other option: a one-time offer after its first passphrase unlock. The exact words come to your phase-4 walk either way.
