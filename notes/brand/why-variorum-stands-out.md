# Why Variorum stands out — the ranked list

The positioning list every piece of marketing draws from, **ordered by
importance** (how much each reason differentiates, not how often competitors
talk about it). Drafted 2026-09-26 alongside the shorts scripts in `video/`.
Each entry: the claim, why it's a differentiator, and how to show it on camera.

---

**1. A paragraph can hold many versions — open, side by side.**
The identity. Every other tool buries whole-document snapshots in a version-
history menu; Variorum holds parallel takes of *one paragraph* open next to each
other, for as long as you want, with the rest of the page undisturbed. No
shipping writing tool does this. *On camera:* the fan bloom + the in-place swap
(short 01's demo beat).

**2. It never destroys your words.**
Not a trash can bolted on — the architecture itself: deletes are recoverable
from the note's tray with Put back; splitting and merging paragraphs are
reversible moves, never rewrites; whole-note versions can be saved and restored;
structural undo/redo exists alongside ordinary text undo. You can be bold
because nothing is irreversible. *On camera:* delete → tray → exact-slot return
(short 03, beat A).

**3. Savepoints — a commit history for your prose.**
Pin the moment a paragraph is good, keep rewriting, restore whenever. Each take
carries its own append-only history — and the app quietly saves for you at the
moments you'd regret most (closing a note mid-edit, a long idle stretch, a big
structural change), so the safety doesn't depend on your discipline. *On
camera:* pin → wreck it → restore (short 03, beat B).

**4. Really fast — at any size, by law.**
Not "optimized": the app has an architectural law that no action may cost more
than what's on screen. A 50k-word manuscript scrolls and types like a tweet.
Apple Notes visibly lags past ~10k words; that failure mode is designed out
here, not patched. *On camera:* the split-screen stunt (short 02); the typing
beat lands hardest.

**5. Private and local-first — you own the file.**
Your whole collection is one encrypted database file on your own Mac. No
account required to write; no server between you and your journal; sync is a
choice, not a dependency — and the file itself is portable: AirDrop it to a
new Mac and open it with your passphrase (13x-gated — verify shipped before
claiming). *On camera:* the AirDrop-to-a-second-Mac move IS the demo if two
machines are handy; otherwise said over the calm page, or the app working in
airplane mode.

**6. Real encryption, not a checkbox.**
The store is encrypted at rest on disk, and sync is end-to-end encrypted — the
server only ever holds ciphertext and can't read a word of your prose. For a
journaling app this is the trust floor, and almost nobody actually clears it.
*On camera:* claim + one still of the database file being gibberish; don't
over-demo.

**7. Calm surface, depth on demand.**
At rest a note is seamless minimalist prose — the variant machinery is summoned
per paragraph, on request, and folds away after. Power tools usually wear their
complexity on every surface; this one hides a database under a clean page. This
is what makes 1–3 livable daily rather than a power-user mess. *On camera:*
every demo's opening and closing frame — the calm page IS the shot.

**8. A true read mode.**
Writing and reading are different acts and get different surfaces: a calm,
book-like page (continuous or paged) for re-reading your work as a reader would
— and clicking any sentence drops you back into editing at that exact spot.
*On camera:* the edit ⇄ read flip on one note, then the sentence-click landing.

**9. Many ways to see the same writing.**
One store, many views: the takes workbench, side-by-side compare, an outline,
a spatial canvas of cards, a blocks power-view. Nothing is copied between them —
they're different lenses on the same text. *On camera:* one distinctive view per
video as B-roll; never tour them all at once.

**10. Books that borrow, never own.**
A note can live in many books at once; smart books assemble themselves from tags
and rules; chapters are real and reorderable. Structure is pointers, so
reorganizing is free and un-reorganizing is too. *On camera:* one note visibly
appearing in two books; a smart book filling itself as tags are applied.

**11. A built-in inspiration pool.**
Scraps, loose sentences, and half-ideas have a first-class home (Snippets), and
dragging a piece into a draft copies it with its lineage — the original stays.
No more "ideas.txt". *On camera:* drag from the scraps column into a draft.

**12. Native Mac craft — and it looks like yours.**
A real Mac app (no web wrapper), system fonts, two designed voices with light
and a fully black dark room, sixteen colour palettes plus custom. It feels like
an instrument, not a website. *On camera:* the theme ripple as a satisfying
B-roll loop; also just the general fit-and-finish in every frame.

**13. A real iOS companion (in progress).**
Not a viewer: capture and edit from the phone against the same store, takes
included — designed as a complete companion, not a lobotomized port. *On camera:*
phone capture appearing on the Mac; hold this one until mobile is demo-ready.

---

Rationale for the top of the order: 1–3 are things essentially nobody else has
(the moat); 4–6 are things everybody claims but almost nobody delivers (the
proof burden — which is why the speed one is a stunt video); 7+ are what make
the daily experience lovable once someone's in the door.
